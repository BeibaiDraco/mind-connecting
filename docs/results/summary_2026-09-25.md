# Summary of results (as of 2026-09-25)

All experiments are complete: three rounds, v1, v2 and v3, each of which first froze the protocol and then ran confirmatory tests on new episodes. The vast instance is paused (stop, not destroyed; it can be restarted at any time). All raw records are on the SD card at `/Volumes/VERBATIM SD/mind-connecting-data/results/`, with record counts and SHA256 checked shard by shard.

## In one sentence

When two copies of the same model are linked: as long as A's attention can read B's memory, A takes B's private information as its own, and does not notice. What decides it is the way the information comes in, not the information itself. It is a source error at the moment of recall: after the link is cut, A remembers clearly the rule it was originally assigned, but a shift remains in "which one do I plan to use now". It is not caused by the grammatical person of the wording. And a two-way link does not make the two copies merge into one.

## Preregistered confirmatory results (600 new episodes each, Holm correction within family, 95% intervals)

**Supplementary descriptive results** (not preregistered confirmatory tests):

- **Strength curve** (300 episodes): claiming rises steeply as the read weight goes from 0.5 to 1, and saturates from w=2 on. At w=1 claiming already reaches +32, while A's ability to state B's rule has risen by only +4.7.
- **Does not notice**: at w=2, 99% of A's open self-reports talk about B's code word as "my code word", and almost never mention B's name. When asked "how many minds", the share answering "two / partly shared" actually falls.
- **Two-way link** (B′ pilot, 80 episodes, did not pass the prespecified threshold):
  - With mutual reading of fixed memories, 94% of pairs "swap".
  - With reading of live states, 43% of pairs have one side winning, and the excess over what two mutually independent sides would give is only +0.07, below the 0.10 threshold.
  - Cutting the link before the question makes all of these phenomena disappear.

## What is new

According to our literature search (each item verified while writing the paper; see `paper/refs_verification.md`), past work that passes internal states directly between models looks only at accuracy and efficiency. We found a risk that nobody has measured: the receiver talks about the partner's information as its own, the source is silently lost, and it does not notice this itself.

The other points are:

- what decides "whose it is" is the way the information comes in;
- this error happens at the retrieval stage, which lines up with human source-monitoring research;
- language tags hold the boundary, while name tags written into memory do not;
- being connected does not mean merging into one.

We make no claims about consciousness, the number of minds, or phase transitions.

## Limitations

- So far there is only one model (Qwen3-4B-Instruct), and only one kind of task. A second model is left for the second arXiv version.
- At strong reading the claiming metric saturates, so the medium-strength comparison was done separately (T3-w1).
- With link-cut answering, M still retains +1.9, which is a very small residue.
- The outcome of a two-way pair is assembled from two separate questions; the protocol already states this.
