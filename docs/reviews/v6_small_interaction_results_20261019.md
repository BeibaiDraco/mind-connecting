## Conclusion

Case1 shows a concrete feedback-dependent forward behavioral response: the focal A beacon flip makes B switch to the correct alternative action, whereas forward cut, return cut and matched return replay remove that switch. A also changes its later action, but both later choices are wrong for its own actual world and lie outside the fixed old/new target pair. It is therefore a coupled-response clue, not a successful task-level return chain. Case2 does not reproduce the correct LIVE forward response; its apparent later change is also wrong. Neither case completes the intended correct LIVE forward-and-return chain.

The findings support further investigation of real feedback effects while exposing a severe task/interface problem. They do not establish reliable reciprocal control, an advantage over information transfer, general superiority to steering, or a population effect. No new GPU run, gain/prompt selection, sample expansion or formal admission follows.

The separate native readout and actual action remain the scored outcomes.

## Implications and next decision

The initial diagnostic fix addressed composing an explicitly supplied fact with a rule. This pilot exposes a separate requirement: the other participant's new private fact must become usable through the channel while each agent retains its own rule and identity. Generated notes show own/other beacon and rule confusion, and sometimes repetitive outputs; these are useful failure traces, not a unique causal diagnosis.

The next proposed small diagnostic should separate current-fact transmission, own-rule retention and action composition in the continuing two-agent history, with matched factual text access as a capability control. A prospective self-state reporting format or timing change may be worth testing, but neither is adopted here. Keep new materials and fixed controls; do not search on the present reciprocity outcome. Establishing those prerequisites would justify another small interaction check before considering formal scale. The previous frozen failures and numerical-admission limits remain intact.
