## Not found, unverifiable, or needing care

1. **Craniopagus twins with a thalamic bridge: no reliable clinical or neuroscientific source.** No peer-reviewed clinical or neuroimaging report on the Hogan twins' neural connection was found. Cochrane (2021, Synthese) says explicitly that no thorough first-hand scientific report exists and relies on television documentaries and newspaper reports, the most detailed being a 2011 New York Times article. Later philosophy discusses the same case (e.g., Kang 2022, Synthese, doi:10.1007/s11229-022-03890-w; not added, and not read beyond its metadata). Recommendation: omit the case, or mention it only as a philosophical example citing `cochrane2021shared`, with hedged wording ("reportedly"). Never state perceptual or neural facts about the twins as established.
2. **LatentMAS proceedings details are unavailable.** PMLR had not published the ICML 2026 volume at the time of checking (its newest volumes are UAI and COLT 2026), so there are no page numbers. Acceptance is confirmed by the icml.cc poster page and the arXiv comment. "Spotlight" appears only in the authors' arXiv comment and GitHub.
3. **Abstract-level checks only:** `cheng2026latentpay`, `turner2023activation`, `zou2023representation`, `yang2025qwen3`, `wegner1999apparent`, `johnson1993source`, and the additions `pham2024cipher`, `zheng2025thought`, `panickssery2024llm`, `binder2025looking`. Before the paper states any number or method detail beyond this table, read the relevant section of the full text.
4. **Preprints that are not peer reviewed:** `flamant2026bicameral` (May 2026), `zhang2026latentaudit` (Jul 2026), `cheng2026latentpay` (Aug 2026), `pearsonvogel2026latent` (Feb 2026), `turner2023activation`, `zou2023representation`, `yang2025qwen3`. Cite them as preprints.
5. **Novelty wording.** The full-text checks found no examination of whether the receiver attributes transmitted content to itself or to the sender in Ramesh & Li or in Flamant et al. None of the other latent-communication abstracts mention source attribution either. This was not an exhaustive literature search, so phrase any novelty claim cautiously ("to our knowledge").
6. **Nothing was fabricated or missing.** Every candidate ID and citation resolves to a real paper on the stated topic.

**Plain-language summary for the PI (additions):** All 35 entries were traced to their original sources; none was fabricated or nonexistent.

Six points must be corrected when the opening is written:

1. **"One of the few, perhaps the only, experiments that can reach the hard problem"**: no literature says this, so it can only be written as the authors' judgment. The citable statement is: "because experience is private, directly connecting brains has been proposed as a route to first-person evidence of another's experience" (Ramachandran & Hirstein 1997; Hirstein 2008, 2012; on the machine side, Watanabe 2022).
2. **"Unlike an ordinary brain–computer interface"** is our own wording, with no citation.
3. **Tononi & Koch**'s "two consciousnesses merging" is a conditional prediction of IIT: it holds only if the integrated information of the connected system exceeds that of each brain alone. It is not an observed phenomenon.
4. **Nagel 1974** only says that experience "seems" to escape objective description; he states explicitly that this does not prove physicalism false.
5. **Chalmers 2023** does not say "we don't know". He thinks the probability that current LLMs are conscious is fairly low, but that future systems deserve to be taken seriously.
6. **Shanahan 2023**'s position is negative, and can only be used as a reminder that "first-person utterances are not evidence".

Two findings useful for the story:

- **Hirstein 2008** predicts that the receiver would experience the other's content but know that it is not its own; IIT predicts that the two minds would be replaced by one merged mind. Our LLM result is a third outcome: taking it as its own.
- **The closest precedents to ours** are Ackerman & Panickssery (ICLR 2025) and Betley et al. (ICLR 2025). In the former, altering the model's internal state while it reads text makes the model claim text it did not write; the latter tested whether a code word is attributed to "self" or to another persona. Neither involves two linked instances or the route by which content comes in, so the novelty holds, but it should be written as "building on this work".

### Wording rules for the introduction and discussion (additions)

- **Hard problem.** No source says that connecting brains is one of the few experiments that could bear on the hard problem. Write it as the authors' view. The citable statement is: because experience is private, connecting brains directly has been proposed as a way to obtain first-person evidence about another's experience [ramachandran1997qualia; hirstein2008mindmelding; hirstein2012mindmelding]; for machines, see [watanabe2022biological]. Chalmers lists the reportability of mental states and a system's access to its own internal states among the "easy problems" [chalmers1995facing], and our measures belong to that class.
- **Nagel 1974.** Experience has a subjective character tied to a point of view, which an objective description *seems* to leave out. Nagel says this does not refute physicalism (p. 446).
- **Ordinary brain–computer interfaces.** The contrast ("there the other end is a device; here it is another subject") is our own framing and stays uncited.
- **What happens when two subjects connect.** State it through the disagreement:
  - Hirstein predicts the receiver would experience the other's representations while knowing they are not his own, and notes that no such experiment has been done [hirstein2008mindmelding].
  - IIT predicts replacement by one merged mind, under a stated condition: the joined system's integrated information exceeds each brain's alone [tononi2015consciousness, note 13; koch2019feeling, ch. 10].
  - Roelofs asks what gradual fusion would be like [roelofs2019combining, ch. 8].
  - Split brains resist a whole-number count of minds [nagel1971bisection, pp. 408–409].
- **Split brain.**
  - Sperry's patients behaved *as if* each hemisphere had its own awareness, after full forebrain commissurotomy [sperry1968hemisphere].
  - Pinto et al. found unified responding in two patients; most experiments used one patient, and the paper was contested [pinto2017splitbrain].
  - A joint review concludes the evidence does not settle one versus two agents [dehaan2020splitbrain].
- **Brain-to-brain interfaces.**
  - Grau et al.: about 2 bits per minute end to end, sent by email [grau2014conscious].
  - Rao et al.: at most 0.05 bits per second by design [rao2014direct].
  - Jiang et al.: one bit per exchange, 81% mean accuracy [jiang2019brainnet].
  - Rats: 62–64% on a two-choice task, with no bit rate [paisvieira2013brain].
  - Reviews call current systems mostly binary [nam2021direct; hildt2019multiperson].
  - Do not call any of these the first human interface.
- **LLM consciousness.** Unresolved. Careful assessments judge it unlikely for present systems while seeing no obvious barrier for future ones [chalmers2023llm; butlin2023consciousness; butlin2026indicators, abstract only]. Cite Shanahan et al. only as a caution that first-person talk is weak evidence [shanahan2023roleplay].
- **Functional self.**
  - Recognizing one's own text, shown against human text [panickssery2024llm; ackerman2025inspection].
  - Describing learned behaviors [betley2025tell] and internal states [binder2025looking; lindsey2025introspection].
  - Keeping the model's omniscient view apart from a character's belief [zhu2024beliefs]. This is not a self-model.
  - These findings come from other models and do not show that Qwen3-4B has these abilities.
- **Novelty.**
  - Internal interventions can make a model claim text it did not write [ackerman2025inspection, "coloring"].
  - Fine-tuned models can attribute codewords to themselves or to another persona, with some leakage [betley2025tell].
  - Write our contribution as extending these to two coupled instances and to the route by which content arrives.
- **Human analogy for the write-time residue.** Only *generative* elaboration (improving another's idea) raised later recall-own plagiarism; imagining did not [stark2005elaboration; stark2006elaboration].
- **Prompt injection.** Retrieved text can carry instructions [greshake2023indirect]. The instruction-hierarchy account is a preprint that was not accepted at ICLR 2025 [wallace2024instruction].
- **KV-channel provenance and security.** These are preprints checked at abstract level only [brito2026latentlie; asif2026lcguard; liu2026beyondtokens].
- **The name "brain bridging".** [koch2020bridging] is science fiction with a factual afterword. Cite it only as the origin of the term.
