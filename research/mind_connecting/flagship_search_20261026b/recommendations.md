# Can a connected model use the possibilities another model did not choose?

**Recommendation for discussion with the PI:** make this the leading new research question. When two frozen LLM instances communicate through native internal states, can the receiver use a donor-supported alternative that the donor did not select, in response to private evidence the donor never saw? The conceptual interest is whether connection gives access to something that remains revisable beyond the sender's current conclusion. This is a concrete candidate for the main project, with a staged empirical test; it is not a demonstrated effect, a certified first-in-literature claim or an adopted experiment.

The question connects the original brain-bridge vision to a tractable aspect of a shared future. A past computation can support several possible next decisions. The sender chooses one; the recipient later faces a different evidential situation. Does internal access allow that recipient to use a different supported continuation, or does it mainly reproduce the sender's selected answer? This operational question avoids making verbal ownership reports stand in for useful cognition. Text can also describe alternatives, and an explicit distribution can be sufficient: the proposal does not depend on declaring language incapable of uncertainty.

## What the closest papers have and have not shown

[Interlat](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.09149v5), §5.2 and Appendix F, explicitly motivates parallel hypotheses and supplies token-distribution and task-behavior evidence. We should credit that hypothesis. The missing test in its inspected comparisons is whether changing a specific unchosen alternative changes a receiver's appropriate response to a later private observation, while the donor's task-optimal leading answer is held constant. Broad probability mass is not itself identification of which semantic alternative becomes useful.

[Confidence Laundering](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.20662v1), Appendices A.8–A.11 and B, motivates preserving uncertainty and alternatives, but its inspected handoff endpoint is a fitted uncertainty predictor. A native recipient using the identity of an alternative for an actual decision is a stronger functional endpoint. [Beyond Final Accuracy](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.01042), §3, already measures actual latent revision and recovery when both initial answers are wrong. Consequently a better final answer or a jointly correct answer alone is not our contribution.

[Dense Latent Communication](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.13594v2) and [LatComm](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.26840) already study private context encoded before future receiver questions. [Hidden Messages](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.28905) already demonstrates causal content substitution and an internal query-and-reply chain. Our gap cannot be simply a late question, useful state transfer, a return path or an untrained implementation. The relevant unresolved implication is **alternative-specific, contingency-sensitive use**, and the distinction between recovering supporting evidence and inheriting an already-processed inference. See the [original-source checks](late-source-checks.md) and [independent screening](screening.md) for exact reading scopes and further precedents.

## A decisive example

Consider two constructed evidence situations. In both, hypothesis A is the best current answer. In one, B is the next plausible explanation; in the other, C is. Their stipulated probability distributions can be (0.6, 0.3, 0.1) and (0.6, 0.1, 0.3): identical winner, winning probability and entropy, different residual possibilities. These are task-defined quantities, not measured model beliefs.

After the donor state is captured, the receiver privately learns that A is impossible. Its appropriate choice is now B in the first situation and C in the second. The donor never sees this new observation. A common answer and scalar confidence cannot distinguish the two situations; a channel preserving the relevant evidence or alternatives can. Additional observations must sometimes leave A appropriate and sometimes favor a different residual option, so neither copying the winner nor always choosing the runner-up solves the family.

Use two separate instances of the pinned Qwen model, with their own contexts, and import a designated real donor KV window. Reuse each captured payload across the receiver's private-observation branches. Change donor evidence separately from the receiver observation, counterbalance names, and score the receiver's committed choice. The actual inference trajectory must record its own committed choice before export; a sibling report cannot establish what that trajectory chose. Task-defined leaders and observed donor choices remain distinct, with all prospectively generated families retained. The entire generated world family is the observation unit; restored branches and multiple choices are not independent samples.

The [development check](questions/alternatives/development.md) and [plan](questions/alternatives/plan.md) specify the finite family, payload timing, actual readouts, comparators and prospective gates. Their synthetic checks establish an information requirement, not model performance.

## What would make the result valuable

The first result to seek is a controlled change in the receiver's decision that follows both the donor's residual evidence and the recipient-only observation. Compare native transfer with no message, irrelevant or counterfactual donor state, the donor's answer, its actual confidence/alternatives reports, an explicit complete-distribution oracle, and full evidence text. Include computation controls. Count tensor bytes and generation costs; a few KV positions are not a few text tokens of bandwidth.

A native result matching a sufficient explicit alternatives report would still establish functional preservation. It would not establish superiority to text. A native result beating only a winner token is insufficient for a strong efficiency or unique-capability claim. Likewise, a useful imported state may simply let the receiver recover premises and recompute. The defensible first conclusion is access to residual alternatives **or their supporting evidence**. Calling this transfer of an already-computed hypothesis space requires the additional evidence-versus-inference comparison in the plan, and even that comparison has stated process-bundle limits.

A strong paper would move beyond a single toy success: establish the specific causal pattern on independently generated material, show how it survives or changes as the donor processes the evidence, and reproduce the relevant functional comparison in a second task family. The first mechanism opportunity is state timing and actual payload substitution, not a speculative self neuron. A comparison of donor processing stages concerns the full induced state change unless a more selective intervention separates its components.

If ordinary native private-fact transfer works but residual-alternative use fails while explicit complete-evidence decisions succeed, the result identifies a useful limit of this interface under the tested conditions. If ordinary native transfer itself fails, the study has not tested the hypothesis. An imprecise null is inconclusive. A finite feasibility pass should precede main collection; its boundaries and unresolved execution choices are in the plan.

## Relation to our existing evidence

Our strongest old findings distinguish access from source reporting and show route-dependent attribution errors and selective recovery. They motivate measuring what transferred state enables a recipient to do, rather than inferring that from what it calls its own. They do not show that an unchosen hypothesis is present, useful or transferable. Current snapshots and native-window operations offer implementation components; they do not establish the new task's success. The main one-way comparison deliberately does not assume a dependable reciprocal chain. Reversing which instance serves as donor can counterbalance roles without asserting that interaction is necessary.

The second candidate—whether a native recipient can retrieve information that matched donor continuations fail to express—remains a promising queue item. Its required fairness ledger and context-reset explanation make it a less economical first investment. The third candidate—useful reliability weighting despite wrong source reports—is retained as a consequence study for the old paper. Neither is silently developed into part of the main experiment. The [candidate record](candidates.md) and [screening](screening.md) preserve their merits and limitations.
