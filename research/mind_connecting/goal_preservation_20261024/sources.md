This refresh concerns the PI's specific question, not reuse of the project's private observations. No external reuse of those observations was newly established. Existing dataset evidence and its independent review remain in [the neutral dossier](../conceptual_inputs_20261024.md) and [review](../conceptual_review_20261024.md).

## Closest ML precedent: legitimate goals already interfere

[Gupta, Krier and Matlin, When Goals Leak Across Relationships](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.17365v1), October 12, 2026; §§3–7. Both coordinator and ML author read these sections. A frozen OLMo model serves two clients with opposing legitimate objectives in one shared conversation/cache. Twenty scenario families include assignment swaps and isolated-client competence controls. Reported deviations include opposite and mixed choices. Goal-span K/V ablations at probe-selected heads shift decisions toward the current client, with random-head/non-goal and reversed-client controls. This already establishes benign cross-client goal interference and causal relevance of cached goal-related state. It does not identify a unique goal-adoption mechanism.

The recomputation path is not token-equivalent, so the authors do not attribute differences solely to cache persistence. Section 7 explicitly leaves preserving useful shared memory while isolating client goals for future work. Preserving initially correct actions after ablation is not demonstrating retained transfer of useful partner-only knowledge. A two-instance version or another model alone would be an extension/replication. The proposed additional question requires useful donor information that cannot simply be discarded; any claimed repair must retain that benefit while reducing interference. An earlier paper's future-work statement alone does not certify a current open problem.

## Other close ML boundaries

[Huzaifa et al., Safety of Latent Communication](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.39788v1), §§2–3, Table 1, Appendix C.5. Coordinator and author checked methods/results; the independent reviewer additionally checked utility-preserving repair in §5/B.4/C.1. Planner/refiner/solver or expert/summarizer agents all receive one query. Benign mathematics training of hidden-state-to-soft-token links, with model weights frozen, increases harmful compliance relative to text while sometimes improving task utility. Refusal-prefix interventions recover much of the refusal behavior. Thus useful benignly trained communication can weaken an existing policy; it is not evidence that the policy was erased. The study does not independently vary two legitimate task objectives. Benign training is also distinct from an untrained interface carrying a nonmalicious donor's separate goal.

[Li et al., Beyond Final Accuracy](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.01042), §§3–4, Tables 1/4. ML author inspected original methods/results; coordinator read the protocol and central findings. Agents independently solve the same question, then revise from fixed trajectories. Correction and preservation are separated, with no-message revision controlling extra reasoning. Richer reasoning messages can increase both correction and corruption; receiver instructions alter the balance. Therefore selective beneficial versus harmful communication and aggregate accuracy's insufficiency are already studied, including latent channels. The proposed distinction is different valid objectives under shared correct facts: both agents can be right for themselves. Answer preservation is not identical to goal preservation.

[KITE: Task-Oriented Key-Layer KV Communication](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.08820v1), §§3, 4.1, 5.2, 5.6/Figure 6. ML author inspected these sections. Four roles solve one query. A layer is selected using receiver trajectory distortion relative to full-KV communication, with common teacher-forced tokens. Receiver-oriented selection and filtering are therefore not new. Its reference already includes the donor's full cache; reproducing that trajectory does not establish preservation of an independently assigned receiver objective. Token-concentration changes are not a demonstrated separation of facts from goals.

[HijackKV](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.19957v2), §§4–7. ML author checked the attack construction and measurements. An optimized prefix changes contextual KV states of a subsequent common benign text chunk, later reused without exposing that prefix. This already demonstrates that identical visible content can carry behavior-changing prior context. Absence of explicit malicious text is insufficient to distinguish a new phenomenon. Our proposed legitimate-goal variation is different from attack optimization, but that alone is not the contribution. Identical factual payload text computed under different goals is a useful control; it fixes visible facts, not the hidden representation.

[When Does Latent Communication Pay?](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.04893v2), audit and Appendix C.2–C.3. ML author checked the mismatched-registry relay. Donor-consistent errors can result from mismatched factual content; the primary interface and latent-only variant differ. This is a direct alternative to interpreting donor-compatible actions as transferred goals.

[Opacity Is Not Privacy](https://aclanthology.org/2026.emnlp-main.2186/), primary indexed abstract only. Both author and coordinator failed to open the full page/PDF. It describes a receiver-conditioned bottleneck, adversarial message/transcript regularization and topology gates that reduce leakage while preserving task utility across private-context QA, reasoning and tool planning. Whether its tasks test independently assigned goal preservation is unresolved. It blocks any broad first-selective-transfer claim, but its abstract does not answer the proposed behavioral comparison.

## Cognitive and neuroscience precedents

[Sowden and Catmur, Control of Imitation](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4380005/), Methods/Figure 1, Results/Figure 2. Cognitive author inspected the primary experiment. Sixteen people performed instructed finger movements while viewing irrelevant movements; imitative and spatial compatibility were independently varied. rTPJ stimulation versus a control site increased imitative interference without a corresponding spatial-interference effect. This motivates controlling the use of competing action representations, not a general self module or an LLM-to-brain-region mapping. Its transferable design move is selective interference against a matched nonsocial alternative.

[Gläscher et al., States versus Rewards](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2895323/), Figure 1 and task/results. Initial transition learning without rewards preceded learning outcome values and free choices. State- and reward-prediction errors correlated with different activity patterns; this is not proof of anatomically independent knowledge and goal stores. The design separates knowledge of how the world works from what outcome is valuable. Transferred information should support a new recipient objective, not just repeat a demonstrated action.

[Najar et al., The actions of others act as a pseudo-reward](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7723279/), Figures 1/4, Methods, Discussion on value shaping versus decision biasing. Consecutive demonstrations and private choices distinguish transient copying from more persistent shaping models. Players explicitly shared symbol values. The authors acknowledge that reward, value and policy shaping cannot be separated behaviorally in their single-step task. This is a strong caution: persistent donor-compatible action is insufficient evidence of transferred goals. Revaluation, new paths and action remapping are motivated comparisons, not automatic solutions to all mediator ambiguity.

[Corcoran et al., When your goals inspire my goals](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7909217/), Study 2 and General Discussion. A Registered Report's three studies found goal inference but did not support the expected behavioral goal-adoption account. This constrains broad readings of the original Aarts goal-contagion studies; it does not establish that all social goal influence is absent. Knowing another's goal and adopting it must be measured separately.

Two partial-reading leads remain explicitly limited. [Dolk et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23339346/) show nonsocial reference events can generate joint-Simon-like interference; the author inspected its abstract and the original Sebanz publisher preview, not Dolk's full methods. [Charpentier et al.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2020.02.028) separate imitation and goal emulation in fMRI studies; full access failed, so whether different assigned goals were tested remains unresolved. Neither is used to certify novelty.

## A computational precedent beyond LLMs

[Barreto et al., Successor Features for Transfer in Reinforcement Learning](https://papers.nips.cc/paper/6994-successor-features-for-transfer-in-reinforcement-learning.pdf), §§2–4, equations 2–4, read by coordinator. Transfer across reward functions with shared dynamics is already a central problem. Successor features separate reward weights from expected feature occupancy under a policy; those features remain policy-dependent. Thus neither knowledge/reward separation nor recombination for a different goal is new in general. The possible contribution concerns whether ordinary goal-conditioned LLM states permit such selective use and what causal failure or remedy they exhibit.

## Search scope and current consequence

Searches covered latent communication with goal conflict/preservation, benign contamination, intent drift, hidden-state misalignment, cache goal leakage, selective receiver transfer, source/control attribution, observational goal learning, imitation control, reward revaluation and successor features. Literal and conceptual equivalents yielded important new neighbors, especially the October 12 cross-client paper. Search terms included `LLM goal leakage`, `latent communication conflicting objectives`, `multi-agent goal preservation`, `latent communication safety`, and human goal contagion/self-other control. Original methods were read for deciding sources as indicated above; abstracts or inaccessible passages remain marked.

The proposed contribution is now **useful compositional transfer under independently assigned goals, with a causal test of unwanted donor-goal influence and retention of information benefit under any repair**. Generic interference, benign policy weakening, selective revision, role binding, or a new interface alone do not establish novelty. No exhaustive priority claim follows. The full privacy paper and goal-emulation study remain unresolved close leads; later work must be checked before publication.
