# Selecting question patterns for this project

October 25, 2026. Applies [bank revision three-lines-patterns-v1](patterns/README.md); independent review and correction recheck completed; see [review](../reviews/patterns.md). This is the additional broad generation batch already authorized in the [brief](../brief.md), not a reset of candidate repair limits or a newly adopted experiment.

The [original vision](../../../../docs/background/Brain_Bridging_to_LLM_Selfhood_Research_Handoff_2026-09-23.md), §§2–5, makes content transfer a prerequisite while keeping control, attribution, asymmetry, recovery and history open. The [corrected evidence](../../gap_audit_20261024/evidence.md) constrains these applications: Qwen's scalar matched-access contrast does not match all information; recovery after cutting does not prove memory restoration; Robin conflict constrains self-exclusive stories; goal/rule reports do not identify action mediators; no reliable useful reciprocal chain is established. Initial V6 diagnostics included committed actions, while the later fixed-scene batch consists of discarded probes. Do not flatten that distinction.

| Pattern and possible opening | What corresponds in this platform | What is missing | Scientific consequence of an answer |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1: Which functions require ongoing connection, and which become locally sufficient? | Saved states, readout cuts and selective START/NOW recovery show the importance of timing and endpoint | Useful new action task, independent checks of retained information and selective interventions; old attribution branches do not establish competence | A functional map can distinguish report-time dependence from acquired useful capability, without assigning a subject count |
| P2: Does responsive coupling reorganize the situations each instance can successfully act in? | Controllable donor/recipient paths and isolated forward effects support a feasible intervention vocabulary | Reliable complete useful return path, fresh perturbations and opportunity logs | Distinguishes passive sequence sufficiency from adaptation across contingencies; can retain collective organization without asserting individual social recognition |
| P3: Does a shared history create a division of cognitive work that is not captured by the current fact inventory? | Independent private histories, copied runtime states and frozen common weights allow controlled histories | Demonstrable contextual role differentiation and responsibility-map use; partner swaps matched for channel quality | Explains when pair organization is history dependent and how it can be preserved or disrupted; no durable learning or psychological continuity claim follows automatically |

These routes differ from asking whether foreign goals overwrite local goals. P3 earns that distinction only if it manipulates a learned responsibility map and measures allocation; an old conflict task with relational wording stays the old argument. P1 with capacity forecasts overlaps the previous forecasting candidate and retains its one consumed repair. The withdrawn broad goal-control argument retains two consumed repairs. No candidate gains novelty from a new name, a philosophical analogy, or an exact task not found in search.

The older [access/control and capacity note](../../paperbank/access_control_and_capacity.md) remains useful for designing separate ability and forecast measurements. It is not sufficient to select P1 over P2/P3, and this author has not newly revalidated its source claims. The present bank expands the question-generating moves rather than ranking the old candidates by default.

Recommendation to the coordinator: screen all three openings for scientific contribution and nearest conceptual precedents. P1 is closest to current intervention affordances; P2 is closest to the original responsive-other question but has the largest demonstrated feasibility gap; P3 supplies a genuinely different conditional question about contextual organization, with no empirical target evidence yet. These are judgments about correspondence and uncertainty, not adoption or acceptance predictions. All need new observations; none authorizes effect hunting in raw completed outcomes.
