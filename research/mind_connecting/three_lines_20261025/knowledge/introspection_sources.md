# Introspection: additional bounded primary checks

October 25, 2026. Root-authored supplementation. These are limits on broad novelty, not evidence that all self-monitoring is impossible. Candidate-specific deciding comparisons still require independent review.

## Lindsey (2025), Emergent Introspective Awareness

[Original article](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2025/introspection/index.html). This refresh read the introduction and main overview; it did not re-audit the full supplementary design. The work tests injected-concept identification before the concept appears in generated text, separation of prior internal content from input, and intended versus artificially prefixed output. It reports conditional, unreliable successes. Thus generic internal-source discrimination and ownership-like judgments already have direct precedents. Nothing here licenses an inference about conscious experience.

## Hahami, Jain and Sinha (2025), Feeling the Strength but Not the Source

[Original v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.12411v1). Read introduction, methods §§3.1–3.5, results §§4.1–4.2 and the summary table. Llama-3.1-8B showed 20% success in the selected original-style detection-and-naming configuration, fragile performance across prompts, and up to 70% classification of four normalized injection-strength categories. These are distinct tasks and selected best configurations; their percentages are not a common sensitivity measure. The paper's “source” does not mean ownership by another independently acting instance. It nevertheless blocks novelty claims based solely on unreliable naming with detectable intervention strength.

## Singh, Linzen and Ravfogel (2026), Can LLMs Introspect? A Reality Check

[Original v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.26242v1). Read introduction, §5.3 methods/results, Figure 3 description, Appendix I and parameter-table entries. The central addition is a textual intervention alongside activation intervention and unperturbed control. Tested models struggle to distinguish the intervention routes, leaving generalized irregularity detection as an alternative to source-specific introspection. The article cannot directly replicate inaccessible Claude models and does not establish universal incapacity. Its table contains varying selected settings and a Gemma model-name discrepancy across two-/three-option rows; this synthesis does not infer an exactly matched global comparison. The deciding relevance is the already-existing three-route source-discrimination design, not a universal negative result.
