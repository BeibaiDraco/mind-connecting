# Additional primary-source constraints from the coordinator

October 25, 2026. These are neutral field additions, not preferred candidate questions. Reading was through original HTML/PDF, with depth stated. No source was reproduced computationally.

**Beyond Memory Majority: Latent-Source Reasoning for Multi-Agent Memory Arbitration (CAMA)**, Lin et al., August 20, 2026. https://arxiv.org/html/2608.19701v1. Read introduction, methodology, main experimental setup/results, Appendix D and metric definitions. The task is external-memory arbitration, not direct hidden-state exchange: multiple entries may derive from one evidential source. The method learns query-dependent memory-to-factor assignments, incorporates provenance priors and learns retrieval/trace/stop actions. Controlled variants augment existing benchmark pools with paraphrases/summaries from a common source and complementary distinct evidence. Evaluation includes replication sensitivity and independent-evidence gain. Thus repeated cross-agent information counted as independent support, and its mitigation, are existing research topics. Recorded provenance provides extra structure, and constructed variants do not establish universal independence inference in native shared-state agents. The distinction between known factor identity and inferred semantic grouping remains important. A new internal-channel implementation alone would not establish an advance.

**More Debate, Same Evidence: Structural Limits of Homogeneous Multi-Agent Groundedness Judges**, Ji, July 31, 2026. https://arxiv.org/html/2608.00243v1. Read setup, §§6–8 and limitations. Three same-model role-conditioned agents share evidence and debate. Main single-agent reference uses a different model variant: gains/losses are whole-system comparisons, not an isolated causal debate effect. Within-panel analyses find correlated errors, little second-round semantic novelty and poorly calibrated confidence. Changing reference model changes some signs. This establishes a close precedent for studying dependence among nominally different agents while also showing why aggregation, model and evidence controls matter. Low embedding novelty does not prove no useful reasoning occurred, and a fixed evidence pool can still support new deductions.

**ECMR: Evidence-Aware Deliberation Control for Multi-Agent LLM Reasoning**, Liu et al., October 13, 2026. https://arxiv.org/html/2610.17869v1. Read §§3–5 and introduction; supplementary protocols not comprehensively audited. A trained external controller over frozen role-conditioned LLMs uses support diversity/independence, contradictions, role overlap, budget and verification history to choose deliberation actions. It explicitly targets confident agreement from duplicated or correlated support. Fixed scheduling and state-shuffling comparisons probe the utility of adaptive organization. Its independence/false-consensus features are operational proxies; they do not identify a native shared-state self model. Main performance claims and formal guarantees are not independently reproduced. This source constrains generic novelty rather than proving our possible question is answered.

**Computing Arm Movements with a Monkey Brainet**, Ramakrishnan et al., 2015. https://www.nature.com/articles/srep10767. Read abstract, introduction and experimental architecture; not every quantitative appendix. Two or three monkeys contribute neural recordings to a common avatar controller, with shared or partitioned control and visual feedback. Coordination improves with prolonged training. This is useful evidence about joint sensorimotor organization, but it is not direct neuron-to-neuron state injection in every monkey and does not show fused identity or experience. An LLM system with frozen weights cannot simply import the biological learning mechanism.
