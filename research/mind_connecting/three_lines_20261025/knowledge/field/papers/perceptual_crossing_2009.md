# Perceptual crossing: interaction success need not imply individual recognition

Auvray, Lenay & Stewart (2009), *Perceptual interactions in a minimalist virtual environment*, New Ideas in Psychology 27, 32–47. [Primary PDF](https://cepa.info/fulltexts/478.pdf), DOI 10.1016/j.newideapsych.2007.12.002. Read: introduction and selected methods/results passages, especially §§2.3–2.4 and 3.1, pp. 37–39; not a full-paper audit. Later perceptual-crossing work was located but not fully audited here.

**Problem and design.** Does recognizing another person depend only on observing their behavior, or can joint activity help constitute successful discrimination? Blindfolded pairs moved avatars in a one-dimensional environment with binary tactile feedback. Objects included the partner, a fixed object and a displaced lure reproducing the partner's trajectory. Encountering the partner stimulated both participants; encountering its lure did not.

**Decisive result.** Most identification clicks followed partner encounters. However, after normalizing by encounter frequency, avatar and lure click probabilities did not significantly differ (§3.1.3). Participants collectively generated more mutual encounters; the authors did not establish reliable individual discrimination of partner from lure on each encounter.

**Use here — our extension.** This is a particularly valuable negative control against turning coordination into self/other understanding. An LLM pair could jointly succeed because the loop creates favorable states while each instance misjudges who contributed. Conversely, accurate partner reports could coexist with ineffective coordination. Testing the relation requires independently recorded joint achievement and individual causal judgments. The lure comparison motivates counterfactual contingency controls, but copying it literally would be premature: our internal channel has no demonstrated reliable complete reciprocal task chain. It also cannot justify expecting a deterministic receiver to react differently to exactly identical inputs.
