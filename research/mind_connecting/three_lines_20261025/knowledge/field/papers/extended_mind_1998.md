# The extended mind: a functional boundary claim, not a fusion result

Clark & Chalmers (1998), *The Extended Mind*, Analysis 58(1), 7–19. [Author's text](https://www.consc.net/papers/extended.html); use the journal metadata for pagination because the author HTML header differs. Read selected passages in §§1–5 on active externalism, Otto's notebook, access/reliance criteria and socially extended cognition; not a complete argument audit. This is a philosophical argument, not an experiment.

**Question and argument.** Why should a cognitive process end at the skull when an external resource performs the same relevant function as an internal component? The paper contrasts mental and external rotation, then compares remembered information with reliable notebook access. Its proposal concerns explanatory roles and enduring availability, not mere statistical correlation. It explicitly extends the possibility to reliance on other people. It does not infer that the participants' experiences fuse.

**What remains open.** Whether causal participation is sufficient for cognitive constitution is a philosophical dispute; ordinary intervention results alone cannot settle that criterion. Reliability, accessibility and standing reliance matter to the argument, beyond one successful request.

**Use here — our extension.** Distinguish an instance gaining an independently retained ability from the connected pair temporarily providing that ability. Disconnecting, replacing the partner, and changing stored histories could separate these organizations. That would describe where a functional capacity depends on current computation, without proving an extended subject. A further question concerns whether an instance appropriately changes its expectations when that organization changes. The previous forecasting proposal already occupies that latter branch and retains its existing review history; renaming it as extended cognition creates no new novelty. A separate descriptive map of retained versus partner-dependent capacities would need its own substantive contribution beyond standard tool-access ablations.
