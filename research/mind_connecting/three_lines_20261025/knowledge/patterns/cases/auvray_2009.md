# Auvray, Lenay and Stewart: separate recognizing an event from creating its occurrence

Malika Auvray, Charles Lenay and John Stewart (2009), *Perceptual interactions in a minimalist virtual environment*, New Ideas in Psychology 27, 32–47, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.newideapsych.2007.12.002), [primary paper](https://cepa.info/fulltexts/478.pdf). Read methods, §§3.1–3.3 and discussion; decisive locators pp. 37–39 and 42–43, Figure 4.

## Source argument

Participants moved in a one-dimensional environment with binary tactile contact. A partner avatar and its offset lure shared movement, but only avatar contact stimulated both participants. Correct clicks favored avatars. Crucially, clicks normalized by stimulation did not significantly distinguish avatar from lure. The authors explain success through interaction producing encounters, rather than demonstrated stimulus-level recognition of the partner. Their trajectory analysis describes attraction toward mutual contact.

## Question formation and proposed answer

Our reconstruction: an apparent recognition success can arise because the system generates a favorable distribution of experiences. The worthwhile unknown is whether performance depends on event interpretation, the process producing events, or both. The source proposes joint exploratory dynamics. The conditional comparison guards against replacing that account with an unsupported individual recognition ability.

## Transfer and limits

Proposed extension: in a decision system, perturb one participant's private observation after a shared checkpoint and compare the other participant's adaptive response under live feedback and a frozen pre-perturbation replay. Record both opportunities for an appropriate response and success conditional on opportunity. If live feedback produces better opportunities without better conditional decisions, that is an interaction effect worth explaining. A conditional-decision improvement asks a different question.

Necessary conditions: an intervention with a known correct consequence, a functioning response path, comparable pre-perturbation histories, and an explicit definition of opportunity. Conditioning on opportunities affected by treatment is descriptive decomposition, not automatically an unbiased direct-effect estimate. A single passively replayed trajectory cannot establish counterfactual responsiveness. Identical receiver states and complete input sequences cannot produce a deterministic live/replay difference. The human task is not evidence that an LLM recognizes another experiencer.
