# Clark and Chalmers: ask what causal role a boundary excludes

Andy Clark and David Chalmers (1998), *The Extended Mind*, Analysis 58(1), 7–19, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/analys/58.1.7), [author text](https://www.consc.net/papers/extended.html). Read §§2–4; publication pagination follows the journal, because the author HTML header gives different pages.

## Source argument

The target is an assumed boundary: why should location inside the skull decide whether a process counts as cognitive? Their mental-rotation cases compare internally and externally supplied operations. Otto's notebook then motivates a stronger claim about standing belief. The argument requires an appropriate functional role, dependable access and integration with action; it is not the claim that every causal influence constitutes cognition. These are philosophical comparisons, not experimental findings. Consciousness is explicitly distinguished from cognition (§3).

## Question formation and proposed answer

Our reconstruction: replace a location-defined category with a question about which operations sustain performance. The authors propose active externalism; an empirical application need not accept that constitutive thesis to test causal dependence. We can ask whether the explanatory organization changes when resources become continuously accessible, while leaving the metaphysical boundary unsettled.

## Transfer and limits

Proposed extension: give a problem solver independently verified access to a remote operation, then compare cutting access before versus after the necessary intermediate work. Add restoration and an equally costly interruption control. A loss only before completion would locate when that operation matters; persistent dependence would motivate a distributed computational description. Equal outcomes could mean internal retention, redundant resources or an insensitive task, not refutation of extended cognition.

Necessary conditions are a useful operation, specified interruption timing, comparable task states and controls for interruption damage. A useless connection or corrupted cache cannot reveal an extended capability. Neither useful dependence nor source confusion establishes a continuing person or a combined subject. The case generates a causal-organization question; it does not promise a new empirical literature gap.
