# Wegner, Erber and Raymond: disrupt organization without treating all memory as interchangeable

Daniel M. Wegner, Ralph Erber and Paula Raymond (1991), *Transactive Memory in Close Relationships*, Journal of Personality and Social Psychology 61(6), 923–929, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.61.6.923), [author-uploaded original](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/21380422_Transactive_Memory_in_Close_Relationships). Read introduction, Method, Couple Recall and the overlap/individual-recall analyses; pp. 923–927, Figure 1. [Harvard PDF](https://scholar.harvard.edu/files/dwegner/files/wegnererberraymond1991.pdf) timed out in direct retrieval; the full author-uploaded text was accessible.

## Source argument

The study recruited 59 dating couples. Participants remained with their partners or were paired with strangers; category responsibilities were assigned or left unspecified. They studied without discussion and recalled individually. Pair recall counted items recalled by either partner. Existing couples outperformed new pairs without assigned structure, with the ordering reversed under imposed assignments. Assigned structure impaired established pairs; its numerical benefit to new pairs was nonsignificant. The supported interaction does not identify its mechanism. The overlap analysis did not support increased duplication as the explanation; individual recall showed a parallel pattern (pp. 926–927). Natural relationship history was not randomized.

## Question formation and proposed answer

Our reconstruction: an organizing intervention can have different effects depending on a prior arrangement. Instead of asking only whether two people remember more, ask what makes the effect depend on history. Coordinated allocation motivates a proposed explanation, but the source does not isolate it. A general application should measure allocation rather than infer it from aggregate performance alone.

## Transfer and limits

Proposed extension: two systems experience a controlled history assigning complementary information domains. Later swap partner identity while preserving the available facts, and separately swap the facts while preserving the partner and expected responsibility map. Measure successful retrieval, duplicate queries and coverage failures. A history-by-reorganization interaction would motivate an account of learned-in-context dependency; content-only effects would favor simpler information availability.

Necessary conditions: demonstrable use of the responsibility map, matched accessible information and verification that a partner swap does not silently change channel quality. An identical pair with no differentiated history has no organization to disrupt. Frozen weights permit context-dependent adaptation but do not reproduce years of human relationship formation. An ordinary address directory could produce the proposed routing effects; these would not establish a uniquely relational mechanism. Preserved performance could reflect successful remapping or broadcast compensation, so routing must be inspected before favoring direct content access. These manipulations and predicted outcomes are our proposed extension, not findings from this paper or from the project.
