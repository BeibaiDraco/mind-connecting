# Three reusable ways to open a question

Revision three-lines-patterns-v1. Each route is a single-case inspiration with an explicit proposed extension. They do not jointly demonstrate a universal theory. Choose the operation from the scientific situation, not from the appeal of the label.

## P1. Replace a location-defined boundary with a map of necessary operations

**Starting tension.** Two systems produce the same answer, but it is unknown where the operations needed for that answer occur or whether the arrangement survives separation. [Functional-extension case](cases/clark_chalmers_1998.md).

**Question-forming move.** Ask what must remain connected, at which stage, for a specified ability. This yields a worthwhile descriptive causal map without requiring a hypothesis about selfhood. Distinguish access needed to obtain information, access needed to transform it and access needed only at final reporting.

**Hypothesis-forming move.** A distributed-operation account predicts selective loss when a needed operation becomes unavailable. A retained-information account predicts preservation once the relevant information has been acquired. These are proposed empirical alternatives for a specified operation, not exhaustive rivals or a test that settles the constitutive philosophical thesis. Retained facts and dependence on remote computation can coexist; retrieval scaffolding and additional compute must be controlled before attributing a unique operational role.

**Decisive operation and normalization.** Start from paired saved states; interrupt before acquisition, after acquisition but before a new transformation, or only at the final decision. Compare each with its own connected continuation and an interruption control. Measure actual success on fresh challenges; do not normalize every treatment to a single mean-access score and call its information identical.

**Essential conditions.** Useful baseline behavior, stage-specific interventions, independent evidence that relevant information remains, and disruption controls. Optional strengths include operation-specific restoration and new examples. A single architecture or human phenomenology is incidental.

**Failed transfer.** A disconnected solver performs worse because its whole cache was erased. This cannot locate a remote operation: the intervention destroyed local evidence too.

**Worked adaptation.** An analyst gets measurements from a partner, then must solve a new query revealed after transfer. Compare a retained dataset, access to a partner who can run a needed computation and a control interruption. Preservation after cutting supports retained sufficiency for that query; selective loss followed by operation-specific restoration supports continued functional dependence. The new project must supply the working task, intervention validity and a literature check. Adding self-capacity forecasts is optional and reuses the older forecasting argument with its existing repair history.

## P2. Ask whether coupling changes the experiences available, the response to them, or both

**Starting tension.** A connected pair succeeds more often, but the success may come from creating different situations rather than recognizing or deciding better within the same situations. [Perceptual-crossing case](cases/auvray_2009.md).

**Question-forming move.** Separate the distribution of encountered decision opportunities from behavior within them. A genuine collective regularity may be interesting even without a stronger individual representation. This opens a positive explanatory route: explain how a stable coordination pattern arises.

**Hypothesis-forming move.** Responsive sampling predicts that a partner adjusts which evidence reaches the recipient after an intervention. A passive-information account predicts adequate performance when those evidence sequences are supplied without response. Neither account entails a subjective social relation.

**Decisive operation and normalization.** Specify the causal path A intervention → B response → changed A opportunity/action. Compare live and frozen replay after the intervention, and report the total effect plus descriptive event/decision decomposition. Use a replay from the relevant pre-intervention trajectory; a replay taken from the successful post-intervention branch already contains the answer to the intervention and answers a different sufficiency question. Conditional event rates require overlap and do not, by themselves, identify controlled direct effects.

**Essential conditions.** Actual committed actions, useful information transmission, a successful return path, recorded opportunities and branch-equivalent initial states. Mere bidirectional wiring is insufficient. Optional strengths include partner replacement and several perturbations.

**Failed transfer.** Two instances exchange identical cached material once and produce correlated answers. There is no adjustable response process, so this cannot test mutual adaptation.

**Worked adaptation.** One agent encounters an unannounced obstacle. Its partner can adjust a later cue in response, and the first must then act. Freeze the cue generator before the obstacle in the replay arm. Better live performance opens the question of which adjustment mattered; unchanged performance suggests this task did not need feedback. A content-matched successful replay tests sufficiency of the realized sequence, not whether live response was dispensable across unanticipated changes. The new project must create useful reciprocal behavior first.

## P3. Turn a history-dependent intervention effect into a test of organization

**Starting tension.** An organizing intervention has different effects depending on prior organization; in the supporting study it impaired established pairs, while the numerical benefit to new pairs was not established. This asks what the pair organized before intervention, beyond what either member currently knows. [Distributed-memory case](cases/wegner_1991.md).

**Question-forming move.** Cross history with reorganization while separating who has facts from who is expected to provide them. The unknown is whether the relationship contains an operational allocation that current content alone does not summarize.

**Hypothesis-forming move.** An allocation account predicts selective disruption when responsibilities change unexpectedly, even when the fact inventory is preserved. A content account predicts losses mainly when facts disappear. Generic unfamiliarity predicts broader swap costs that do not depend on the learned allocation.

**Decisive operation and normalization.** Establish equivalent task competence and matched exposure. Cross prior coordinated versus independently assembled histories with preserved versus permuted responsibility maps. Hold the union of available facts fixed, check each side's accessible facts, and compare coverage, duplicated work, retrieval routing and actual success. Baseline differences require within-history contrasts; raw post-swap means are insufficient.

**Essential conditions.** Evidence of responsibility-map use, history that can create complementary roles, controlled channel quality and no covert extra information. Optional strengths include restoration and transfer to a new topic. Human intimacy and years of learning are not transferable requirements.

**Failed transfer.** Reset all histories and ask whether the model still prefers its old partner. The reset removed the hypothesized organization; a null result says nothing about its earlier role.

**Worked adaptation.** Two frozen-model instances accumulate different evidence classes across a short context history, learning where to request each class. Swap their partner-facing addresses while keeping each fact set intact, then restore the map. Selective routing failures and recovery would implicate an operational directory, including an ordinary address lookup mechanism. Unchanged performance could reflect direct access, successful remapping or broadcast compensation; inspect routing to distinguish these possibilities. This supplies a new question about organization only if it measures responsibility allocation. Recasting old donor-goal interference as “relationship organization” does not create a new argument or reset its repair allowance.

## Choosing and rejecting a route

Choose P1 when timing of dependence is the unknown, P2 when responsive interaction may produce the relevant situations, and P3 when a controlled prior history may organize distributed work. A project can combine them only after identifying which contrast answers which question. None requires a claim about consciousness, a privileged self module, or a universally optimal connection strength. A descriptive null or conditional advantage can be informative if the necessary conditions were met; failed prerequisites remain failed prerequisites.
