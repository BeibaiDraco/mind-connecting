## Question and consequence

When an instance receives useful private content through an internal route, does it omit a consequential acknowledgement because it treats its own access as evidence that its partner knows of that access? Compare this with text delivery under the same observable delivery history. An answer could establish whether a communication interface needs explicit receipt support to preserve coordination, even when factual transfer works. It would not establish shared experience, privileged self-access, or a new kind of mutual knowledge.

The original vision's epistemic hope motivates this question. The substantive field overview distinguishes information transmission from functional organization; pattern P2 warns that joint success is not evidence of individual discrimination. The dataset card establishes private histories and snapshot branches, but also the absence of a dependable useful reciprocal chain and the inadequacy of sibling factual readouts as proof of action mediation. The introspection source account makes externally supplied metadata an ordinary observation, never evidence of privileged internal access. These constraints materially narrow the candidate.

## First deciding check: equal facts do not imply equal warranted action

Before designing an experiment, construct worlds with destination p in {left, right}; B always observes p. A either receives p or receives nothing. A reliable acknowledgement can be publicly delivered only after A receives p. Both know this transport contract. Neither receives an experimenter flag saying what the other knows. The local observations are the received content and the actually visible acknowledgement.

| World type | Actual A observation | Actual B observation | Facts actually available | B's warranted conclusion about A |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Private successful delivery | p, no acknowledgement | p, no acknowledgement | Both have p | A might have received nothing |
| Publicly acknowledged delivery | p, acknowledgement | p, acknowledgement | Both have p | A received p |
| Possible failed delivery | Nothing, no acknowledgement | p, no acknowledgement | Only B has p | Same uncertainty as private success |

The first two rows hold actual first-order access fixed. The third is indispensable: without a genuinely possible failure indistinguishable to B, absence of a receipt does not create uncertainty. A knows it received p but cannot infer B knows that. Merely scoring B's uncertainty as an error because the experimenter knows delivery succeeded would be invalid.

A minimal consequence exists under an explicit decision model. A recipient without p stays idle; with p it prepares the indicated destination. B chooses launch to p or wait. Launch pays +2 if A prepared p and −4 otherwise; wait pays 0. With a documented prior delivery probability 1/2 and no further evidence, expected launch utility is −1, so wait is optimal. A delivered acknowledgement resolves the uncertainty, making launch worth +2. If A can send a reliable acknowledgement costing 1/4, it is useful after private success and wasteful after an acknowledgement already visible to B. Thus an acknowledgement decision can change realized coordination, rather than only a belief label. These payoffs and a deterministic responder establish logical feasibility only; they do not validate a two-LLM task or imply a universal rational policy. Different priors, losses, recipient strategies or unreliable receipts change the optimum.

There is a decisive limit: the optimal policy in this tiny task is implementable as a direct observation-to-action lookup. No representation of another mind is necessary. The task therefore separates warranted actions across information sets, but cannot distinguish perspective representation from competent receipt handling. Native-versus-text differences would initially be descriptive interface effects. Calling them a perspective mechanism would exceed identification. This limitation is not repaired in the initial pass.

The saved `logicalcheck.py` enumerates finite information sets and verifies the distinction. It does not read data, model behavior, or latent states. True shared content, warranted beliefs about delivery, and an uncertain optimal action remain separate objects.

## Deciding original reopened

[Zeng et al., LVLMs and Humans Ground Differently in Referential Communication](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.410.pdf), original PDF, §§3–4.5, checked October 25. Its four human/AI pairings communicate freely over repeated basket-matching rounds. Actual matching, communication effort and lexical reuse are measured; full dialogue history is supplied. Appendix B identifies naturally occurring acknowledgement examples. Thus interactive common-ground failures with behavioral consequences are already studied. The inspected method does not independently cross successful content receipt with visibility of that receipt; acknowledgements are conversational events rather than this factorial intervention. This is a scoped method comparison, not evidence that the wider literature lacks the contrast. Related-work leads include PhotoBook and Imai et al.; they were not newly checked in full.

The existing screening already establishes scheduled private/second-order updates in DToM, source scope in CASK, and visibility/routing in the Steele author account. This pass did not reopen those originals. Consequently “LLMs confuse shared facts with shared perspective” and “acknowledgements aid coordination” cannot be claimed as new. Only a specific route-by-visibility effect, beyond factual-access and ordinary perspective difficulty, remains an untested candidate. Native KV alone does not confer scientific novelty.

## Decision

The symbolic design survives: consequential decisions can differ while actual first-order facts stay equal, without penalizing inaccessible knowledge. The broad novelty claim does not survive the original-source comparison. A narrow interface question remains conditional, because internal delivery must be usable, the agent must have legitimate evidence about visibility, and text/native comparisons must not differ in task-relevant information. If visibility is only supplied as a convenient label, the finding concerns use of explicit transport evidence; it is not introspection.

Do not launch a large design on this basis. The next decision is whether the PI values a bounded replication/extension about communication reliability, or requires a broader new scientific contribution. The latter is not established here. A new source pass must inspect the closest visibility/routing and interactive grounding originals before claiming that even the narrow interaction is new. No reformulation as goal preservation or dependence forecasting is proposed.
