## The one limited-development question

**When internal transfer gives an instance useful information, does it also make the instance incorrectly assume that its partner knows who received it—and can this mistaken assumption change their actual coordination?**

The comparison must hold the relevant first-order facts available to both instances fixed while changing who could observe the delivery/receipt. Compare private delivery with delivery followed by a genuine observable acknowledgement; use a source-tagged text route as a comparison. Both parties knowing a fact does not imply either knows that the other received it. This familiar logical distinction is the design requirement, not the discovery.

A concrete possible setting is a coordinated launch: B may proceed only when it has evidence that A received the current destination; A must predict whether B will proceed. The destination is identical and actually available to both in paired branches, but B has or lacks evidence of A's receipt, and A has legitimate evidence about that visibility. “Proceed” versus “wait/request acknowledgement” must have a task consequence. This example still requires a full information-set table: merely instructing A which answer to select would collapse it into following a receipt label.

The scientific payoff would be to distinguish useful private access from a mistaken inference of shared epistemic access in the same working system. A selective coordination error despite preserved first-order competence would locate a practical failure of perspective management; accurate coordination despite internal exchange would constrain the stronger concern. Acknowledgement rescue could identify missing informational support, but would not alone prove a special self mechanism. The candidate must survive equally informative text controls and ordinary source/scope errors before claiming a route-specific effect. Accurate sibling reports do not show which representation caused an action.

**Closest comparisons:** DToM already schedules private belief updates and asks second-order questions. CASK addresses licensing scope; Li addresses source roles and downstream reasoning; the Steele author account includes visibility-dependent beliefs and routing. These substantially narrow the contribution. The remaining proposal replaces annotated mental-state truth with controlled delivery/observation histories and tests consequential decisions under a factual-access/receipt-visibility separation. Whether that is already covered by interactive common-ground work remains unresolved. Merely adding native KV to a standard false-belief task would be insufficient.

**First uncertainty, before major investment:** construct the smallest explicit information-set worlds and reopen the closest interactive common-ground methods. Show that (a) each required factual fact is the same, (b) the permitted observation history changes the relevant second-order inference, (c) a real action must differ, (d) no hidden oracle receipt flag provides the answer, and (e) the result would change more than our estimate of generic recursive-question difficulty. Stop if the task reduces to source-tag recall, if the model has no usable channel, or if nearest work already establishes this relation and no worthwhile replication target is named. The existing failure of sustained native reciprocity is a feasibility concern, not evidence against the question; this candidate need not start with a long reciprocal game.

## Review accounting and handoff

Candidate 1 concerns epistemic visibility rather than adopting another's goal or forecasting one's own dependence. It may start with an initial independent review and at most two substantive repairs if development shows it is genuinely distinct. This is an allowance, not a promise to spend all cycles. Changes that return to old goal protection or dependence forecasts inherit their existing counts. Other distinct new questions would likewise need initial review plus at most two repairs; relabeling is never a reset.

The useful queues are controlled task-interdependence in partner history, responsive opportunity creation, and bounded self-versus-record refinement. Their deferral is due to unresolved value, source coverage or prerequisites, not proof that the scientific questions are closed. The coordinator should present the enlarged conceptual space and the direct precedents together. The honest present outcome is a sharper next uncertainty to resolve, with the possibility that no new flagship survives.
