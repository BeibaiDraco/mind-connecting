# Intended user-facing scientific summary

The project should recover a broader organizing question: when two initially separate cognitive systems become internally connected, how do their information, division of work, dependencies and continuity change, and do their accounts of those changes remain accurate? This is a research program, not a newly certified paper claim. The previous local-goal-protection framing is one practical case within it.

The original vision matters because the location of a cognitive process need not coincide with an obvious physical or software boundary. Psychology distinguishes remembering content, remembering its source, knowing what another knows, sharing a task and predicting one's capacities. Neuroscience motivates varying the function and direction of connections rather than treating every connection as equivalent. The LLM platform permits exact state copies, controlled private histories, substitutions and cuts that are difficult in brains. Those are tools for studying functional organization, not evidence about consciousness or personal identity.

The literature already establishes much of the broad territory. BrainNet demonstrates limited decoded neural communication and differential source reliance. Perceptual crossing distinguishes interaction-level outcomes from individual recognition. Transactive-memory work makes prior organization relevant to memory. In current AI, LACO studies useful internal sharing with retained local control; Gao et al. directly study partner-specific history using frozen-model swaps and notebook interventions; collective coordination and duplicated-evidence problems also have direct precedents. Thus novel implementation or philosophical vocabulary alone cannot supply the missing scientific contribution.

Our experiments give useful clues. Strong attribution changes with fixed donor memory mean that a responsive second process is not required for that phenomenon. The Qwen KV/STATIC contrast motivates separating factual access from attribution, within incomplete information matching. Selective recovery and Robin conflict require multiple measurements and generic-conflict alternatives. The small V6 results do not establish a dependable useful reciprocal chain or enacted goal transfer. The next question need not reuse the old tasks.

There are four distinct directions worth understanding: whether shared facts preserve accurate perspectives on who knows what; whether common history organizes who performs which work; whether interaction creates useful opportunities that passive content cannot supply under comparable resources; and what newly acquired competence remains after separation. None is an untouched broad field. Their value depends on a causal or descriptive comparison that changes what the closest work tells us.

We tested the first candidate as a deciding exercise. A minimal receipt-visibility design is logically valid, but a simple receipt-to-action mapping solves it. Existing interactive grounding research already studies behavioral common ground. This task could investigate communication reliability; it does not yet justify a new perspective mechanism or flagship paper. It should not be presented as the chosen topic merely because it is easy to formalize.

The most faithful program-level focus remains reorganization of cognitive work and dependencies. A concrete unresolved causal issue in the closest partner-history paper is whether required interleaving itself creates partner-specific coordination costs, or whether complexity and convention divergence explain the association. That could be investigated with carefully matched tasks, but its originality, importance and feasibility remain to be assessed. Responsive opportunity creation and post-separation competence remain distinct queues with their own literature and interface prerequisites; the latter retains the earlier capacity-family review history.

The recommendation is therefore a broader scientific frame and a bounded next source/design decision, not a claim that a new top-conference topic has already been secured. The three-line assessment and annotated reading route are saved for the PI, including original links, known overlaps, failed interpretations and precise remaining uncertainties. No protocol, model, training, GPU run or manuscript change was adopted.
