# What should connecting two cognitive systems let us discover?

The strongest organizing question is how connection changes the organization of knowing and acting: which information becomes usable, which computations depend on the other participant, and whether each participant accurately represents that changed relationship. This restores the PI's original vision without requiring the old assignment task to remain the center. It is a research-program question, not an already novel paper claim. The completed experiments provide useful constraints but have not established a stable new collective capacity.

The philosophical motivation is unusually clear. A physical boundary makes it easy to assign a thought or memory to a person before considering its causal organization. Internally coupled artificial instances let us vary access, dependencies and history while holding weights fixed. Psychology supplies distinctions among source memory, perspective, distributed expertise, prediction and control. Neuroscience supplies the question of how connection function, location, direction and time affect organization; counting shared signals is insufficient. These motivations do not make an LLM experiment a test of conscious experience or personal identity. The [field account](knowledge/field/overview.md) connects each motivation to original sources and their limits.

## What the three lines establish together

The original question “whose past is it after we connect?” has several empirically different readings. Historical origin is fixed by the record. A memory's information can become usable elsewhere. Its causal contribution to a later decision can change. A system can correctly or incorrectly describe any of those relations. No one answer substitutes for the others. The future-facing counterpart is which information and computations support the next action, and whether the agent correctly anticipates the other side's participation. These are experimentally tractable questions about continuity and perspective; numerical personal identity is not the outcome.

The literature already occupies several tempting broad claims. [LACO](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.22504v1) studies useful internal sharing with local control, [Goals Leak](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.17365v1) studies cross-client goal interference, and [CAMA](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.19701v1) studies duplicated evidence. [Testing Interchangeability](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.05279v1) directly perturbs partner-specific history in frozen-model teams. [Emergent Coordination](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.05174v3) studies complementary collective organization. Their scope does not exhaust the original vision, but a native KV implementation of those questions alone is not a distinctive conceptual contribution. [Detailed comparisons](screening.md).

Our evidence supplies four constraints on interpretation:

- Fixed donor memory can produce large attribution changes without a responsive donor. Misattribution alone is not evidence that a new interactive cognitive organization formed.
- Qwen's closer mean-access KV/STATIC contrast motivates separating content access from source assignment; it does not match every relevant information dimension or generalize to every static intervention. Llama's formal match failed.
- Different post-cut recovery and the Robin controls require separate measurements and generic-conflict alternatives. They do not establish multiple independent self mechanisms or an exclusive self vulnerability.
- Early V6 included committed actions, but no dependable complete useful reciprocal chain; the later fixed-scene batch comprised discarded probes. Correct goal reports with wrong rule responses do not establish goal takeover or action mediation.

These are reasons to choose better questions, not a requirement to make old effects the new paper's central result. Numerical details and original report links are in the [dataset card](knowledge/dataset/card.md) and [verified evidence account](../gap_audit_20261024/evidence.md).

## Candidate scientific questions

| Question | What an answer would change | Current position |
|---|---|---|
| When direct sharing makes a fact available to both, do the agents accurately track the other side's access and the visibility of that access? | Tests use of evidence about a partner's receipt beyond factual access; can characterize consequential coordination errors without identifying a perspective mechanism. | The deciding check passed logical feasibility but reduced the minimal design to a conditional reliability extension; not selected as a flagship. |
| Does shared history organize who performs which cognitive work? | Tests whether current facts alone explain competence, or whether an acquired allocation matters. | Valuable scientific topic, but partner swaps and task/partner-memory interventions already exist. A new causal contrast is needed before prioritization. |
| Does a responsive partner help by creating informative situations rather than improving decisions within fixed situations? | Gives a positive account of what interaction contributes beyond stored content. | Closely aligned with the original vision; active sensing/coordination precedents and missing useful reciprocal execution are substantial prerequisites. |
| What remains locally usable after a jointly derived insight is separated from the interaction that produced it? | Distinguishes retained generalizable information from ongoing distributed support. | Worth keeping, but overlaps the earlier capacity-dependence family and inherits its review history. Ordinary tool removal or answer retention is insufficient. |

The [candidate account](candidates-round-2.md) gives rival outcomes and measurements. The separate [screen](screening.md) assesses those candidates alongside the coordinator's early ideas and late-found precedents. None is an adopted study. Balanced self/other record conflict remains an optional way to sharpen the old manuscript, rather than the required center of this broader program.

## Selection and deciding check

Screening selected the mutual-access candidate for one inexpensive deciding check. The [development](questions/mutual_access/development.md) and [conditional sketch](questions/mutual_access/plan.md) now complete that check. A finite-world enumeration proves that two agents can actually have the same fact while differing in warranted knowledge of receipt, and that their optimal coordination action can differ. However, an ordinary receipt-to-action lookup solves the minimal task. The original [interactive grounding study](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.410/) also already measures common-ground behavior with consequences. The candidate therefore remains a narrow communication-reliability extension; it is **deferred as a distinctive flagship**, not promoted through a more philosophical name. Its logical feasibility does not establish novelty, useful native exchange, or a perspective mechanism.

The scientific program should stay centered on **how connection reorganizes cognitive work and dependencies**, with attribution and self-prediction as possible measurements of that organization. This is an organizing preference faithful to the PI's vision, not an unreviewed replacement protocol. It can accommodate a positive finding about complementary competence as well as a negative finding about interference. A report of ownership need not be its main endpoint.

One concrete unresolved causal question is visible in the nearest partner-history paper: are partner-specific costs caused by the need to interleave work, or by task complexity and divergent conventions that covary with that need? Gao et al. expressly treat their coupling gradient as observational because harder levels also change complexity. A controlled comparison could teach something real here. It must avoid building obligatory communication into the answer: equalizing component workload, information opportunity and resources, then measuring acquired routing/coordination costs, matters more than calling the task a brain bridge. Whether that refinement has sufficient conceptual payoff for this paper remains unestablished. It is the next source/design audit to consider, not a developed recommendation.

The responsive-opportunity question is a second useful queue: under what conditions does ongoing interaction produce task-relevant situations that an equal-resource passive source would not produce? The current source base leaves its nearest active-sensing comparison unresolved, and the platform lacks a dependable useful native return chain. These are two separate uncertainties. A failure of the existing bridge cannot settle the general question; a live-over-replay advantage alone would not settle its novelty.

The post-separation question preserves the PI's “future belongs to whom?” motivation: which new abilities remain available to each separated history? It inherits the prior capacity-family review history. A new answer must go beyond removing a remote resource and observing performance loss. Joint derivation, retention, retrieval assistance and continuing computation are different explanations.

**Recommendation:** adopt this broader organization-and-continuity framing for discussion, keep the above questions distinct, and assess one causal contrast against its closest precedent before choosing the paper's claim. No new flagship is certified by this pass. The old goal-control framing is not mandatory, and the old experiments are clues rather than a compulsory narrative. No expensive collection is justified by the current minimal receipt task.
