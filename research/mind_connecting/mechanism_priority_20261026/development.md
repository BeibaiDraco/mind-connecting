## First deciding check: does the response map identify the explanation?

No. Let A's committed component be `a∈{0,1}` and B's calibration be `c∈{0,1}`, so B's button `b` measures `b XOR c`. Correct complementary sampling gives `b=1 XOR a XOR c`. Semantic following gives `b=a XOR c`. Exactly the same semantic-following response arises from each of these ordinary failures:

- B believes A measured `1 XOR a`, then correctly chooses its complement.
- B knows A's component but uses the reversed calibration `1 XOR c`, then correctly chooses its complement.
- B knows both facts but applies identity instead of the complement operation.

Button following, `b=a`, also admits a fact-error model: a belief `a_hat=1 XOR a XOR c` followed by correct complementary action gives the same button in every world. Thus complete cue-by-map response tables cannot identify literal copying, even with perfect sibling factual reports. Those reports run a different question computation; they do not establish the facts used by the actual action branch. No outcome-conditioned selection or larger sample removes this structural ambiguity.

The surviving question is therefore **which incoming commitment changes the recipient's actual measurement policy, at what processing stage, and with what consequence for the evidence the pair can obtain?** This is a causal question about coordination, with internal explanation as an additional tier. It remains useful without claiming a neuron-level mechanism.
