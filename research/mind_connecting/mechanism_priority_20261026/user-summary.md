# Assessment for the PI

My preferred first small investment is a native-state experiment organized around: **After I receive your internal thinking, can I still discover what you have not discovered?** Both participants are instances of the pinned Qwen model and communicate through real internal K/V states. Text is only a comparison. This is about their ability to produce complementary new knowledge; it makes one part of the philosophical future/independence question measurable without equating a behavior with a human self.

The proposed comparison is unresolved in the originals inspected: does a partner's pre-observation commitment make the receiver coordinate complementary measurements, follow the partner's button, or follow its semantic target? A private cue intervention and a public reversal of the receiver's instrument map predict different actual choices and evidence coverage. Stage gates distinguish choosing what evidence to obtain from using evidence already obtained. The primary route is A-to-B before measurement and B-to-A afterward; it is two internally connected LLMs, but the minimal experiment is staged, not spontaneous continuous reciprocal adaptation.

The broad claim that sharing can reduce exploration is already known. ArcticSwarm measures evidence gathering and integration and obtains 82.6% versus 78.8% on 830 questions with versus without gated isolation. Interlat already evaluates latent communication in action tasks and reports exploratory behavior, using success and trajectory length. The Weight of Teamwork separates produced candidates from final selection. Therefore neither a new benchmark nor the same phenomenon observed through KV would establish conceptual novelty. The gap is the precise causal response and its consequences; its larger significance remains to be earned. Independent review defers this as the main flagship while conditionally supporting a bounded diagnostic. I do not currently have enough evidence to certify an eye-opening, defensible flagship gap.

Concrete task: the team must find a hidden two-component code. Each may measure one component. A privately receives an availability cue and commits a measurement. Its early message is captured before any hidden value is revealed. B receives that native state and commits a measurement using either an aligned or reversed public instrument mapping. Only then are observations revealed, and B may return its observation internally to A for the joint answer. Use native actual-cue and opposite-cue donors, explicit commitment text and no informative source; hide the later gate assignment during acquisition. Record all measured and imported facts. A forced-complementary arm independently tests useful late transfer without selecting successful spontaneous trials.

Proposed sequence: offline leakage/action audit; one 16-layout-family competence/native-transfer batch; if that passes, one 24-new-family causal diagnostic. The latter has 384 receiver action endpoints and 768 final-answer endpoints in its primary grid, not that many independent samples. Work/query and scaffolding diagnostics use the prospectively fixed first eight families, adding 320 action endpoints. Other mandatory controls still need a complete workload ledger and cost estimate. These are development caps, not formal sample sizes or permission to run. Failure of useful native transfer or transparent task competence ends this candidate test; no automatic tuning loop follows.

If a reproducible cue-dependent effect exists, compare different real donor state windows at the identical receiver prefix, remove them and restore them. This can establish a causal input/stage contribution. It cannot by itself distinguish a wrong belief from literal intention copying or identify a pure policy representation. Mechanistic localization is optional; reliable prospective behavioral explanation and consequential fresh-task predictions are the core scientific test. A second wiring-table family is currently only a close-relative extension, not broad generalization.

The strongest outcome would be a precise account predicting when useful internal sharing supports complementary inquiry and when it redirects it toward redundant evidence, with a controlled way to distinguish those conditions. The prediction must hold beyond the exposed toy and matter for the resulting information and success. A known timing benefit, generic impairment, verbal ownership change, or isolated copying effect is insufficient. Successful complementary coordination is an equally valid outcome and would reject the proposed duplication concern in this regime. Existing experiments motivate stage-specific intervention but do not show this effect or reliable useful reciprocity. My recommendation is to buy this specific piece of evidence before betting the paper; not to present the current toy as a flagship already found.
