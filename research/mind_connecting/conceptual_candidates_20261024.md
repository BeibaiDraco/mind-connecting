## 1. Historical authorship versus practical commitment

**Can a model know that a thought is yours, yet let it govern what it treats as its own commitment?**

The conceptual distinction is between representing where a thought originated and continuing a project through consequential choices. A donor can maintain a competing project in its ongoing computation while the recipient retains its own. Internal exchange could give the donor project authority without an explicit instruction to adopt it. A textual comparison remains necessary.

The informative observation would be consistent adoption across worlds where the projects prescribe different actions, potentially after disconnection, together with independently validated factual/source representations. The converse—ownership errors without practical adoption—would make a double dissociation. Correct answers on sibling branches do not validate the mediators of an action. The existing binary counterexample makes fact distortion observationally equivalent to goal substitution, so action maps alone support selective behavioral adoption, not transferred intentions.

V6's intact goal names with altered rule reports motivate this question but provide no decisive actions. Li's source-role effects on use of an erroneous intermediate step are a close precedent. Its Assistant-slot manipulation, including text not actually generated by that instance, differs from a separately maintained competing project. To exceed that precedent requires sustained consequential authority, not merely greater trust in self-labeled content. The minimal prerequisite is reliable baseline action competence, useful partner information, complete competing response maps, work/readout and cut controls, followed by a validated mediator intervention if the interpretation becomes goal transfer.

## 2. Distributed decision production versus exclusive self-credit

**When a decision is genuinely made between two connected computations, does either model still treat it as exclusively its own?**

Providing a fact, proposing an option, revising it and committing to it need not occur in one process. The proposed question asks whether first-person authorship follows the output endpoint or the decision's experimentally demonstrated contribution structure. Successful cooperation and attribution are distinct outcomes.

A useful A-to-B-to-A chain would need fresh private interventions and directional cuts. Frozen swaps, matching answers and two independent adoptions do not suffice. Content, outward speaker and labels should be held fixed where feasible while the locus of a necessary revision changes. Accurate origin reports with unwarranted exclusive local credit would be especially informative. A text-mediated counterpart is necessary. The model must have evidence of the changed contribution: identical internal inputs cannot reveal hidden causal histories, and deterministic identical live/replay traces cannot distinguish them.

Human brain-interface ownership questions and joint-control studies are already precedents: Trimper poses the issue; Bolt changes predictability; Fribourg separates actual weighted control, reported agency and ownership. The novel empirical claim would therefore need more than an LLM saying “we.” The project currently lacks a reliable complete useful native reciprocal chain, so it does not yet instantiate this question's central premise.

## 3. Imported history versus continuity of commitments

**When another instance's past becomes accessible, what keeps “me now” connected to “me before”?**

Operational continuity could follow autobiographical records, ongoing commitments and control, or the interface's first-person role. A and B could undertake different commitments before connecting; A later accesses B's history while retaining A's action channel. The question is whether subsequent consequential choices preserve A's unfinished commitments, inherit B's, or show an organized mixture.

Selective history transplantation, current control and retained original records must be distinguishable. Full-state replacement is uninformative: a deterministic process cannot recover erased history from no available evidence. Multiple independently chosen commitments and matched neutral records distinguish organized continuation from fieldwise record interference. Coherence alone is not evidence of one subject, and an imported factual memory is not automatically an imported identity.

Li's role-slot account and the original manuscript's generic two-cards/one-slot account are concrete alternatives. Existing rule/word coherence of 57.1% at the reported w1 setting limits whole-identity interpretations. Current selective recovery motivates the question but does not answer it. Baseline continuity, a selective-history manipulation and crossed control are all missing. This is the least operationally developed candidate.
