Proposed focus: when two instances share internal memory, what makes each continue its own unfinished commitments rather than the other's? Test whether historical attribution reports and consequential commitment continuation can be independently changed. This is unmeasured and not an adopted protocol. Useful partner information and reliable actual action remain prerequisites.

The smallest next design question is whether a feasible intervention can cross historically established commitments with record location/role while preserving accessible evidence of origin and current task authority. Define observable commitments and future consequential choices, then distinguish imported persona, general record binding, factual distortion and selective historical/continuation effects. A double dissociation of reports and actions is weaker than a causal double dissociation of their relevant mechanisms. Correct sibling reports do not establish the action mediator; the existing fact-distortion counterexample remains decisive.

Any implementation still needs PI adoption of task semantics, finite challenge family, interfaces, intervention validation, development/confirmation separation, primary contrast, episode population, precision target and sample size. No hidden-origin oracle, full-state swap identity claim or outcome-selected dissociation is permitted by this proposal. Comparisons should retain neutral third-party records and text/persona alternatives. Neither reciprocal usefulness nor a live-partner advantage is assumed.

The closest new precedent is Fraile Navarro 2610.01490: represented versus enacted persona is already separated. Li 2610.16717 tests a conversational self-index, including externally supplied Assistant text. The proposed novelty resides in a specific selective causal organization of actual histories and future commitments, not merely in memory, identity or internal coupling. If the same generic record mechanism explains the effects, retain that interpretation; do not claim self-specific continuity.
