## Q1. Does shared information retain two usable centers of action?

**Plain question.** When two instances know the same relevant facts through an internal connection but act through different, changing action mappings, can each use the shared facts from its own current position while also correctly deriving the partner's action? Does the connection preserve, exchange, or collapse these two mappings?

**Knowledge at stake.** Information sharing need not erase the distinction between where a fact came from and which action it warrants for each recipient. The consequential unknown is whether internally imported content supports *multiple correctly situated uses*, or whether its useful meaning remains tied to the sender's center. This asks about the transformation between evidence and action, rather than whether an assigned goal survives interference. It is an operational meaning of perspective; it does not measure phenomenal first-person experience.

**Proposed comparison.** Give each instance the same goal and a different action-to-world mapping. Arrange cases where an identical world fact warrants different actions for the two actors. Establish the mappings through actual action/outcome histories, then independently vary the current actuator mapping and the descriptive actor label. From paired saved states, compare internal sharing with a source-tagged factual input and a single-instance two-actor task. Test both own committed action and a separately scored prediction of the partner's appropriate action. Include neutral, named-controller versions with the same relational structure. A finite architecture contrast should separate an imported observation from an imported already-centered action recommendation; an arbitrary depth sweep would conflate these.

**Conditional hypotheses and outcomes.** A recipient-recentering account predicts own actions follow the current actuator mapping while partner predictions follow the partner mapping, including after labels and actuator mappings disagree. A carried-center account predicts a specific sender-frame error that reverses when the sender's mapping reverses. A generic role-binding account predicts the same errors in neutral-controller tasks and dependence on labels or positions. These mechanisms can coexist. Successful dual use would show differentiated functional perspective despite shared access; systematic carried-center errors would identify a limitation of this interface. Correct answers in sibling readouts cannot establish the mediator of an enacted action; causal interpretation needs separately justified interventions on the relevant mapping information.

**Closest actual work and remaining delta.** [Paul et al. 2026](https://lapaul.org/papers/Reverse%20engineering%20a%20self.pdf), PDF pp. 10–18 and 25–26, already formalizes active centering, actuator discovery and recentering, with computational models and human avatar experiments. Merely discovering one's controller is therefore insufficient. [LACO](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.22504v1), §§3–5, already tests useful sharing while preserving ego control and finds architecture dependence. The proposed additional comparison concerns whether *one shared item can be transformed correctly for two causally established centers*, with sender-center and actor-label reversals distinguishing specific errors. [Li et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.16717v3), §§3.1.3, 3.2.6–3.2.7 and 6.3, already establishes role-indexed attribution/use; its assistant-slot text was not generated by the tested instance. Actual action history would make causal grounding testable here, but does not itself establish novelty. Perry's indexical argument motivates this comparison; Cappelen–Dever's alternative makes ordinary relational computation a substantive explanation, not a nuisance to be dismissed.

**Origin and use of preparation.** Directly generated from the field's distinction between access, self-location and agency. P2's perceptual-crossing case warns that coordinated success need not imply either instance recognizes its own causal position. The data card supplies cloning and actual-commitment affordances, while its V6 failures prevent assuming task competence. Neither the old source report nor NOW is the proposed measure.

**First affordable decisive check and failure condition.** First construct a small symbolic table crossing actor labels, causal mappings and fact origins. Verify that recentering, sender-action copying and label binding make different predictions without changing the common goal. Then compare that exact contrast to Paul and LACO's methods before proposing execution. A later separately authorized no-channel/explicit-fact prerequisite must establish reliable own/partner action mapping. If the only separating effect is foreign-goal takeover, this is exhausted goal-control 2; if all contrasts reduce to the same generic two-role lookup, the claimed special perspective contribution disappears. A functional transformation result could still have value, but that requires screening rather than a new label.

## Q2. Can a connection redistribute privileged access to another process's impending behavior?

**Plain question.** Before either instance commits an action, can receiving its partner's current internal state make the recipient better at predicting the partner's next behavior than the partner is at predicting itself—and what information would explain such an advantage?

**Knowledge at stake.** A functional first-person privilege is a relation between a predictor and a target process. It need not remain attached to historical identity when internal information becomes shareable. The question is whether prediction advantage tracks the process doing the predicting, possession of predictive state, or merely the amount of available evidence and computation. This concerns access to a particular impending behavior, not confidence in general competence or a forecast of connection dependence.

**Proposed comparison.** Fork a target from a saved predecision state: one branch takes an actual action; separate branches produce a prediction of a prespecified property of that action, rather than its entire verbal output. Compare self-prediction, partner prediction before/after receiving target state, an exact self-clone predictor, and an observer given the full visible target history with matched prediction resources. Query both directions across balanced episodes. A no-transfer and irrelevant-state transfer distinguish task information from nonspecific perturbation. Keep the predictor's own next-action task separate to detect simple adoption of the target's action policy. Exact clone predictions are an equality/reference condition, not evidence of another subject.

**Conditional hypotheses and outcomes.** A portable-state account predicts that prediction gain follows access to target-specific state and can be expressed without the recipient adopting the target's own policy. A situated-computation account predicts a residual self advantage after the specified state/history/resource controls; such a result would remain conditional on the completeness of those controls. A generic observer account predicts no advantage beyond complete visible evidence and matched recomputation. An apparent gain accompanied only by target-policy copying would fail to demonstrate a distinct predictive use. Neither successful decoding nor a verbal claim to know another mind establishes introspective phenomenology.

**Closest actual work and remaining delta.** [Binder et al., Looking Inward](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13787v1), §§2.1–2.2, already tests privileged self-prediction using trained self/cross predictors and behavior in separate contexts. The proposed delta is whether access privilege can change hands through current-state communication between frozen instances, with explicit self-clone and equal-computation comparisons. It is not simply repeating self-versus-other accuracy. [Lindsey 2025](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2025/introspection/index.html) shows internal intervention-dependent detection/authorship reports; [Lederman–Mahowald](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.05414v1), §§3–7, and [Reality Check](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.26242v1), §5.3, show why anomaly detection, correct content identification and internal-versus-textual source discrimination must be separated. Their detection paradigms do not by themselves answer predictive access to an independently continuing partner. Role-index effects in Li remain a competing explanation for a self-framing advantage.

**Origin and preparation.** A field-generated extension of privileged-access methods; not a mandatory pattern application. P1 contributes only the instruction to distinguish possession of state from continued dependence on another operation. The dataset permits copied predecision histories but contains no validated native self-prediction outcome. The absence of useful reciprocity does not logically block a one-way prediction comparison, although useful target-state transmission remains an essential missing condition.

**First affordable decisive check and failure condition.** Audit the proposed fork ordering on paper: the prediction must concern the untouched action branch, and the answer property must not be explicitly present in the transmitted history. Check whether full visible history plus equal recomputation necessarily suffices in the chosen task. If so, that comparison may quantify computational savings but cannot establish an unexplained self-access privilege. No new target effects should be inspected to choose the property. Without a reliable untrained prediction baseline this remains a method-development proposal. If it becomes connected/cut capability forecasting, it inherits capacity/portability 2 and must not be reopened under an introspection label.

## Q3. When sharing becomes recursive, can the pair distinguish new evidence from its own evidence coming back?

**Plain question.** If a private observation travels from A to B and returns to A through internal state, can the pair treat it as one evidential contribution while still combining genuinely independent observations from the two sides?

**Knowledge at stake.** Shared content and independent viewpoints are different resources. Two agreeing outputs may express two observations or repeated circulation of one. The consequential uncertainty is whether internal sharing preserves the evidence ancestry needed for calibrated joint inference, particularly when each instance can accurately name the immediate sender. This would connect a functional boundary to epistemic independence rather than to pronouns or subject count.

**Sketch of comparison.** Hold the reported claim and its immediate source fixed while crossing independent versus copied observation histories. Use a small generative environment with known observation likelihoods and externally logged ancestry, and score a real decision whose appropriate threshold differs between one and two independent observations. Include source-tagged text and an explicit centralized ancestry ledger as explanatory controls. Distinguish source identification, content access, numerical calibration and actual action; a source label is not evidence of independence. Feedback/replay comparisons must reflect different ancestry histories, not allegedly different meanings of identical effective states.

**Plausible outcomes.** Correctly discounted circulation alongside useful independent combination would demonstrate source-sensitive evidence use. Confidence escalation or different decisions under recycled evidence would show a specific limitation of shared reasoning. The same failure under ordinary text would support a broader evidence-tracking explanation. No subjective unification follows from either outcome.

**Closest inspected work and open preparation.** [When Latent Agents Lie](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.28958v1), §§3–8, already studies split evidence, full-KV exchange and utility after handling suspect contributions; [CASK](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.09008v1), §§3–6.6, already separates payload from licensing scope; Li already distinguishes role-indexed use. They prevent treating generic provenance-sensitive collaboration as new. The proposed comparison separates trustworthy repeated evidence from trustworthy independent evidence, rather than truthfulness or authority. However, distributed-estimation common-information problems and multi-agent evidence double counting are likely closer methodological literatures; they were not substantively prepared in this fixed reading set. Therefore **candidate development is partial**, and no claim of a literature opening is made.

**Origin, feasibility and first check.** This is a direct epistemic extension of shared access versus separate perspective; P2 contributes the need to track how opportunities/information were generated, and P3 warns that facts alone may omit organizational history. The existing corpus records source assignments but not this independence ground truth. First compare the proposed crossed ancestry manipulation against primary work on distributed evidence aggregation and latent-agent evidence accounting. Only after that could a symbolic two-observation check and a one-way baseline be useful. A full circulation experiment requires the currently missing useful return path. If the question reduces to alternatives reuse or generic source attribution, it retains those existing arguments rather than acquiring a new contribution.
