# Three concrete opportunities after connecting two systems

October 26, 2026. These are ranked research opportunities, not adopted protocols, observed findings or novelty certificates. The PI requested concrete flagship possibilities supported by close readings. The most promising organizing question is: **when two systems share internal information, how does that change what each can do independently, and what remains specific to working together?** This gives the original question about whose past and future an individual possesses a functional, testable meaning.

My conceptual priority is opportunity 1, formation of partner-specific dependence. Opportunity 2 offers a more compact initial causal diagnostic. Opportunity 3 is closest to the question of what survives separation, but its closest-method overlap and causal-role interpretation are less resolved. They are alternative paper centers, not three claims to combine into one overextended paper.

## 1. Does a shared past make participants more interchangeable or more dependent on each other?

The interesting uncertainty is not whether replacing a teammate can hurt. It is whether the *organization of previous work* creates later partner specificity, even when the replacement has the same underlying model, component competence and current facts. Direct access could standardize expectations and make substitution easier; repeated interleaving could instead establish efficient but partner-specific handoff routines. Both outcomes are plausible and consequential for the meaning of an individual's competence.

**What the closest original did.** [Gao et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.05279v1) form frozen-model teams over ten episodes, retaining task and partner notebooks, then compare placebo, role-matched experienced swaps and other memory/replacement conditions. Eight teams yield four independent swap pairs per setting. In one high-coupling setting, progress changes from 63.2 to 58.0, while messages per progress rise from 4.33 to 6.53. This is intervention evidence for partner-specific costs. Their task-coupling gradient also changes task complexity, so it does not isolate the causal history that produces those costs. [Detailed original-method comparisons](questions/interdependence/source-evidence.md) include Shih's task/convention modules, Other-Play and MATE. MATE already shows that shared internal attention can facilitate coordination with unfamiliar partners; its trained policies are a useful opposing precedent.

**A concrete experiment.** Give both agents the same four-step symbolic computation and public tables. During formation, distribute the steps as `A–A–B–B` or `A–B–A–B`, with mirrored roles and balanced operation exposure. Later give all cohorts the same mixture of new tasks. In each cohort, compare the original pair with an experienced, role-matched replacement, using the same reset and notification. The decisive quantity is the difference in replacement cost between the two randomized histories. This separates past interleaving from current interleaving. The first implementable study uses explicit text packets and a bounded persistent notebook; earlier transcripts and task caches are reset and current ownership instructions replace obsolete ones. It is a text/notebook study of formation, not yet a native internal-state experiment. Extending the comparison to native caches requires a separately validated useful channel and an explicit native-state retention contract.

The primary scope is learned handoff efficiency under an imposed partition. A serial four-step task does not automatically give cooperation an advantage, nor does assigning operations demonstrate emergent division of labor. Full-information individual competence, actual committed requests/intermediates and resource accounting are required. A later targeted intervention changes a particular anticipated handoff while preserving its content; history-specific disruption plus restoration is stronger evidence than generic deadline failure. A public handoff contract can test whether the resulting dependence is portable.

**What would be learned.** A formation-by-replacement effect plus a selective handoff explanation would show which past interactions produce present dependence, and what must be preserved when moving an agent to a new partner. A compact conventional explanation is a valuable result; no special collective substance is required. Conversely, useful formation with a precisely small replacement cost would challenge the expectation that repeated coupling necessarily makes partners less replaceable. The contribution must be this cause-and-portability account, not “LLMs have conventions” or the same swap task implemented with KV.

**Main risk.** The controlled task may never develop useful history dependence, and our native channel has not shown reliable useful reciprocity. The next investment is a finite task/carrier audit and a separately proposed competence pilot, not a large formation study. Human interdependence experiments also predate this project; original-method access for some close human work remains incomplete.

## 2. Can sharing improve the use of evidence while reducing the ability to obtain different evidence?

The question concerns independent inquiry. Two participants may become better at answering with the information already collected while becoming worse at choosing complementary next observations. This distinguishes the benefit of sharing a past observation from its effect on the future observations the pair will produce.

**What predecessors actually establish.** [Bernstein, Shore and Lazer](https://www.pnas.org/content/pnas/115/35/8734.full.pdf) randomize human triads to constant, every-third-round or absent peer-solution exposure during 17 rounds of a 25-city traveling-salesperson task. Triads find an optimal solution in 48.3% of trials with intermittent access versus 33.3% under constant access, while retaining stronger average performance than isolation. This directly precedes the broad sharing/exploration tradeoff. Recent LLM diversity and exploration studies further preclude a first-ever sharing-versus-exploration claim. Receiver-conditioned latent selectors and state-aware controllers already adapt communication to current needs. The proposed increment is an experimentally identifiable loss or gain of *new evidence*, with a test distinguishing copied action labels from copied information targets. Read the [question-specific source table](questions/responsiveness/source-evidence.md) for design, sample/unit and evidence limits.

**A concrete experiment.** Both participants must identify the same hidden two-bit code. Each may operate one of two instruments. A's left/right instruments measure the first/second bit. A receives a new private availability cue and commits its measurement choice; B must choose the complementary measurement. Capture the early message before either observation value is revealed, so wrong-cue controls cannot accidentally import an unmeasured bit. Randomly make B's public, calibrated instrument map aligned or reversed. Identical button choices produce redundant evidence under aligned maps and complementary evidence under reversed maps. Copying a semantic measurement target, rather than a button label, predicts a different label response after reversal. Fixing A's committed probe avoids confusing reciprocal revisions with copying.

Independently vary A-to-B connection before B's instrument selection and the B-to-A return connection after the observations arrive; the late assignment is hidden during acquisition. The former can alter which evidence exists; the latter can only alter how available evidence is combined. Record committed probes, exact distinct facts obtained and final code accuracy. Include ordinary text communication and an explicit full-evidence competence control. Different generated answers, verbal claims of independence and different button labels do not themselves establish independent evidence.

**What would be learned.** A selective early-stage loss of evidence coverage with a late-stage gain in answer accuracy would reveal a concrete failure hidden by ordinary fixed-input evaluations. Mapping reversal and private-cue interventions could distinguish stipulated button-following and semantic-target-following response maps. That behavioral separation does not identify a literal copying operation or internal representation. Ordinary copying is a valid causal explanation if the interventions support it. If early sharing instead promotes complementary sampling, that constrains the concern and identifies a useful regime. The potential conceptual contribution is how a channel changes the diversity of future information, not just the agreement of present answers.

**Main risk.** A two-instrument task might merely reproduce already known correlated search. Stronger value requires the stage and mapping predictions to explain a consequential effect, survive generic disruption controls, and replicate in a second evidence-acquisition family. This is a compact candidate for a first diagnostic, not proof that native sharing inherently destroys independent thought.

## 3. After a shared solution, who can still solve a new problem alone?

Two agents may both possess all the input facts and see the same successful output, yet differ in what they can subsequently do without the partner. The specific opportunity is to ask whether allocation of integration work determines that later portability.

**What predecessors actually establish.** [Hendel et al.](https://aclanthology.org/2023.findings-emnlp.624.pdf) extract a task activation using demonstrations and a dummy query, then insert it into inference on a new query with no direct demonstration access. Across 18 tasks and seven models, it retains roughly 80–90% of ordinary in-context performance. [ExpeL](https://arxiv.org/html/2308.10144v3) and CLIN already retain derived textual knowledge and use it on later tasks without actor-weight updates. Thus source removal, new applications and frozen weights are not new by themselves. [Source detail](questions/separation/source-evidence.md).

**A concrete experiment.** A initially has one arbitrary symbol mapping and B another. Both then receive both full mappings. Randomly assign who composes them and who checks the work, with fixed work allowances; both receive the same actual final outputs, including errors. Cut the connection and test each on inputs unused during practice. Reverse the roles across matched worlds. The primary contrast is whether being assigned the integration role improves later solo application. Continuation branches ask whether access to the partner masks unequal local competence.

The initial contrast identifies the integration/checking role bundle. It does not isolate a hidden learned representation from persisting role instructions, different generated text or effective computation. The proposed cache-position interventions test the contribution of retained positions, while re-prefill tests the sufficiency of visible text. Restoration alone establishes reversibility in that context; cache positions are not semantically pure fact/rule compartments. Further mechanism claims require stronger identification than the present sketch.

**What would be learned.** A reproducible role-dependent handoff gap would explain why successful teamwork does not ensure that both participants leave with usable competence. Symmetric fresh-task performance would show portability under the tested conditions. If ordinary text reconstruction removes the asymmetry, that is an informative, practical account. Neither outcome alone establishes a novel form of learning. The included logical check explicitly proves that retaining a rule and retaining premises with recomputation can yield identical new-case answers.

**Main risk and rank.** Self-generated-example work is a close comparator, and the role-history/current-role distinction needs resolution before a strong mechanism claim. This is third priority and more exploratory than the first two. The inherited substantive-repair allowance is exhausted; do not manufacture another flagship by renaming retention, forecasting or selfhood. Keep the total role-bundle opportunity conditional and defer claims that exceed it.

## How the existing experiments help without dictating the paper

The old evidence gives useful constraints, not preliminary confirmation of these hypotheses. Fixed donor KV can alter source assignment without an answering partner, so attribution changes alone do not establish new interactive organization. Closer mean-access matching in Qwen and unequal post-cut recovery motivate separating information access from behavior and persistence, but do not equate all information or prove restored identity. Robin controls require ordinary record-conflict alternatives. Correct goal reports alongside wrong rule reports do not identify the policy used in an actual action. Early V6 included committed actions but did not establish a dependable complete useful reciprocal chain; the later fixed-scene batch was discarded probes. [Verified evidence account](../gap_audit_20261024/evidence.md).

Philosophically, the proposed change is from assigning ownership of sentences to examining continuity of capacities and dependence. Psychology supplies transactive memory, differentiation and transfer as predecessors, not just evocative analogies. Neuroscience motivates manipulating direction, timing and the organization of connections rather than treating more shared signal as more integration. No LLM result here decides consciousness or the numerical identity of persons.

For a competitive paper, a compelling outcome would link a causal manipulation to a change in independent competence or information production, discriminate at least one serious alternative, and reproduce the explanation across fresh task families on the permitted model. A single source-confusion effect, a generic cut penalty or a new benchmark would not suffice. No conference outcome is guaranteed, and no model, training, selector, sample size, GPU expenditure or frozen protocol change is adopted.
