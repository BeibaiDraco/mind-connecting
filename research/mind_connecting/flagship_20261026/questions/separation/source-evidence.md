## What latent collaboration actually establishes

[LatentMAS, v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.20639v4), §3.2, Table 1, and evaluation overview were inspected. The scientific question is whether agents can reason and exchange working memory in latent space efficiently. A sender first generates latent steps; its complete layerwise cache, including prompt and latent-thought positions, is prepended to the successor's cache. Benchmark questions are the evaluation observations, with accuracy and runtime comparisons to single-agent and text-agent systems. The inspected table includes gains and losses: Qwen3-4B GSM8K is 88.2 versus TextMAS 89.8, whereas MBPP+ is 73.5 versus 69.8. These are useful within-problem transfer results, not a factorial test of post-separation abstraction, retained premises and subsequent fresh application. Its cache already contains both supplied information and computed intermediates. A “cut” that leaves this cache does not remove the transferred information. Sequential transfer alone is therefore a direct precedent, not the proposed contribution.

## Frozen agents already retain and reuse derived knowledge

[ExpeL](https://arxiv.org/html/2308.10144v3), §§4.1–4.4, 5.1–5.4 and Table 1 inspected. It asks whether experience can improve later decisions without parameter updates. Successful/failed training trajectories generate textual insights; new tasks receive those insights and retrieved successful examples. Four-fold evaluations cover HotpotQA, ALFWorld, WebShop and transfer to FEVER; outcomes are task success. Insight-only and retrieval-only ablations already separate two memory forms. In HotpotQA→FEVER transfer, source trajectories are not retrieved; GPT-4 adapts source insights using target demonstrations, while GPT-3.5 acts. Reported FEVER success is 63±0.4 for ReAct, 65±1.7 for transfer adapted without target demonstrations and 70±0.7 with them. These are across-fold standard errors, not our episode estimator. Derived-rule transfer to fresh applications, with the original interaction absent, is already studied. Textual updating of insights is not weight training, despite the paper's “finetuning” terminology.

[CLIN](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.10134), Algorithm 1, §§3.2–3.3 and 4.2/Table 1 inspected. It asks whether a frozen language agent can improve across trials and generalize across tasks/environments. A memory generator updates English causal abstractions from trajectories and reward, and meta-memory selects knowledge for new settings. ScienceWorld tasks/environment variants are evaluated over repeated trials, scored by simulator reward. Table 1 reports Base 48.6, transfer to a new environment 52.7, and transfer followed by adaptation 69.5; the latter includes additional experience and must not be called transfer alone. Thus newly applicable contextual knowledge without changed weights is established as a class of behavior. This does not establish a joint latent representation or our model's capability.

## A latent rule without its demonstrations is also precedent

[Hendel, Geva and Globerson, *In-Context Learning Creates Task Vectors*](https://aclanthology.org/2023.findings-emnlp.624.pdf), §§2.2–3.4, pp. 9319–9321, inspected. The question is whether in-context learning separates into a query-independent task representation and its application. The authors extract an activation using demonstrations plus a dummy query, then patch it into another forward pass containing only the new query. This prevents direct attention to the demonstrations. They compare regular ICL, patched inference and no-demonstration/no-patch inference on 18 single-token tasks across seven models; layer choice uses development accuracy. Patched performance retains approximately 80–90% of regular ICL accuracy, versus 10–20% for baseline. Many tasks use already pretrained relations, so this is not blanket evidence of learning arbitrary new rules. It nevertheless defeats a generic novelty claim based on transient activation, absent source examples and novel query alone.

[Todd et al., *Function Vectors*](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.15213), §2.3 and the §3 task/model setup inspected. It extracts task-conditioned attention-head averages, tests restoration under corrupted input-output examples, and combines high-effect heads into a function vector. More than forty simple tasks and multiple decoder-only models are involved. The contents list includes portability, robust input forms and vector algebra; those later results were not closely inspected here and are not used as established findings. This is a consequential remaining lead before claiming that a portable jointly formed operation is novel. A latent-state ablation must distinguish carrying an operation from merely restoring accessible evidence; a head's causal influence does not guarantee a uniquely abstract representation.

## Social influence followed by transfer has an explicit precedent

[Social Catalysts, Not Moral Agents](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.02598), Methods and Phase 2 results inspected. It varies three model architectures, cooperative-bot fraction, visibility and horizon: 108 game sessions, ten agents, ten interaction rounds. Round 11 retains only a final belief summary and changes to a one-shot game with nine strangers and no anchors. Prior anchor fraction has no significant main effect on final investment; a GPT-4.1 public-visibility/20%-anchor interaction remains. This is already a comparison of behavior during interaction and after transition. However, changed partners, horizon, incentives and compressed history are bundled. It does not identify whether a connection cut erased learning. Lexical/sentiment changes and a nonsignificant reasoning-drift statistic do not establish absence of internalization or cognitive restructuring; those are stronger author interpretations. The episode/session nesting also matters. This paper constrains broad novelty but does not settle the proposed state-sufficiency question.
