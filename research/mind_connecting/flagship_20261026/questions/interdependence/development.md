# Decision: develop acquired dependence, not another swap gap

October 26, 2026. Initial independent review completed; **repair 1 consumed**, independent recheck completed with conditional support. Assigned family: causal interdependence and partner-specific organization. Root owns governance and git. This is a proposal, not a research decision or permission to execute.

## Question and investment-changing check

**Does repeatedly dividing a computation across two systems make their later competence depend on their particular shared history, even when the current problem, available facts and component skills are the same?** More precisely: does formation that repeatedly crosses the A/B boundary produce a larger later partner-swap penalty than formation that batches the same operations within each side, and is that acquired cost carried by anticipatory handoffs under the imposed partition? The minimal task tests learned handoff efficiency, not emergent work allocation or serial-chain speedup.

The affordable first check was the direct precedent. [Source evidence](source-evidence.md) shows that frozen identical models, experienced swaps, partner-note removal, efficiency costs and lead-seat asymmetries already exist. Further sources establish task/convention separation, transfer of conventions and shared internal signals for unfamiliar partners. Accordingly, “partners become irreplaceable,” “native coupling creates organization,” and “more interdependence produces more coordination difficulty” are insufficient flagship propositions.

The surviving explanatory increment is **historical causal placement of work**, tested on a common later task. It is not the currently required number of messages. History is randomized; current test requirements are held common. Conventions remain legitimate mediators. The aim is to discover whether a particular opportunity for repeated cross-boundary anticipation creates reusable but fragile handoff expectations, and to identify a specific action-level route. Hollingshead's original abstract already describes experimental interdependence shaping memory organization; this is not the first causal-organization question. The narrower formation-to-replacement-to-repair comparison must be checked against its unread full methods. Native implementation would give the original brain-connection vision a concrete functional object only if these stronger observations are obtained.

## A counterexample that limits the claim

Consider processors with a public algorithm and a learned one-bit send/wait convention. A pair succeeds because the sender volunteers the intermediate on its agreed tick and the receiver waits until that tick. A swapped member uses the other tick, producing a miss; a one-line schedule instruction restores success. More opportunities during formation can increase the probability of adopting such a private schedule. This produces history-dependent swap costs, preserved isolated skill, unchanged fact inventory and rescue without anything beyond an ordinary convention. Thus the proposed factorial alone cannot establish a new kind of collective cognition. Identifying how a known convention mechanism is acquired can nevertheless be the substantive contribution; an exotic mechanism is not a prerequisite for paper value.

A second counterexample is a fixed public schedule used by every processor. Alternating ownership forces more crossings, yet swapping processors changes nothing. Therefore a history-by-swap effect is not mathematically guaranteed by the task graph. This is an analytic counterexample, not a simulated/model validation.

## Why the revised comparison is worth keeping

The original vision asks whether connecting internal processes changes where effective work is organized. A useful answer can be that connection history induces anticipatory handoffs, or that repeated exposure standardizes expectations and keeps partners replaceable despite extensive interleaving. Both are plausible: more crossings provide more opportunities for private conventions, but also more feedback about a public correct procedure. They make opposite predictions about the history-by-swap contrast, despite similar formation gains. Both have consequences for whether we should explain competence by component ability plus information inventory, or additionally by an acquired matching between policies. The substantial positive package would connect a randomized historical cause, held-out performance/efficiency, and a specific intervention on actual scheduling. A bare swap penalty is a replication-level observation.

The three preparation streams change the design: the field account prioritizes organization without subject-count inference; pattern P3 requires measured allocation and P1 requires selective operation interruption; the dataset card supplies snapshots but warns that discarded reports are not action mediators and useful native reciprocity is absent. Existing source-attribution and disconnection findings are motivation only; no old effect estimates answer this new question.

## Disposition

**Conditional research opportunity; credible causal extension, flagship status unestablished.** It is worth a bounded design/feasibility assessment if the PI values the specific acquired-organization claim. Do not treat the whole field as closed, or convert the causal extension into a first-discovery claim. Native feasibility, the action-level intervention, and the unresolved closest human history/interdependence sources remain necessary checks. The [plan](plan.md) identifies what would strengthen, narrow or kill the contribution. No extra candidate generation or exhausted goal-control argument is restarted.
