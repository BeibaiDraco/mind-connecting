# Partner-specific organization: deciding original sources

October 26, 2026. Primary-reader scope: original methods/results below, with explicit exceptions. No model execution or outcome reanalysis. This extends the [previous source record](../../../three_lines_20261025/knowledge/late_coordination_sources.md), preserving its historical reading scope. Search terms included partner-specific conventions, task interdependence, cross-play, transactive memory, and later zero-shot coordination. Absence below means not found within this bounded search, not established absence from the field.

## The direct frozen-model precedent

[Gao et al., Testing Interchangeability in LLM Agent Teams, v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.05279v1), §§3–4, Tables 1–6 and Figure 2. Question: what formation benefit depends on the particular teammate? Same frozen model and role; ten formation episodes produce task/partner notebooks. Eight teams per setting; six forked conditions include placebo, experienced swap, cleared-partner-note swap, intact amnesia and naive replacement. Ten evaluation episodes per condition retain adaptation; independent swap units are four paired teams, not eighty episodes.

Table 1: high-coupling progress 63.2 placebo versus 58.0 swap; messages/progress 4.33 versus 6.53. Recovery of efficiency trails score (Figure 2). Table 2's normalized swap residue is 0.015/0.053/0.081 across low/high/Hanabi. Tables 4–5 examine signal conventions, timing and initiating roles. This is direct intervention evidence for bounded partner dependence, not merely a correlation. Formation-task interdependence also changes recipe/equipment complexity (§4.1); its gradient is observational. Notebook deletion changes length; surface signatures do not uniquely identify mechanisms. The paper already covers history, swaps, conventions and asymmetric coordination. The new question must explain how dependence is acquired, beyond replicating that existence result.

## Conventions and transfer were already separated

[Shih et al., On the Critical Role of Conventions in Adaptive Human-AI Collaboration](https://arxiv.org/html/2104.02871v1), §§4–6, Figures 5–10. Question: can task knowledge and partner conventions be separated for adaptation? Learned task and partner modules are trained with marginal-best-response regularization. New-partner adaptation reinitializes the partner module and trains both; new-task transfer freezes partner modules while adapting the task module on some partners. Tests use contextual bandits, block placing and simplified one-color Hanabi. Figure 9 reports adaptation curves; Figure 10 reports new-task transfer with familiar conventions. Several transfer tests use hand-designed partners to ensure conventions persist. The supporting human experiment has 23 pairs, five tries per training context, and first-try transfer scores: preserving optimal-action sets aids transfer (Figure 5). This is experimental transfer evidence, not native frozen-model organization, and it directly defeats generic novelty in separating task skill from partner conventions. Numerical curve values were not digitized.

[Hu et al., Other-Play for Zero-Shot Coordination](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v119/hu20a/hu20a.pdf), §§6.3–6.5, Table 1, Figures 4–6. Question: can independent agents coordinate without agreed arbitrary symmetry breaking? Train under randomized equivalent relabelings. Twelve models per method span four architectures and three seeds; each cross-play cell averages 10,000 games. Hanabi cross-play rises from SAD 2.52 to SAD+OP 15.32; with the auxiliary grounding task, 17.65 to 22.07. Self-play stays around 24. Twenty nonexpert humans also play both selected bots, with deck/order controls. Convention mismatch can be extremely consequential despite excellent individual/self-play capability. These are training-method comparisons, not isolated causal effects of formation interleaving. Cross-play cells share trained policies and are not all independent samples.

## A newer direct internal-signal counterweight

[Benintendi et al., Grounded Joint-Attention Other-Play, v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.06025v1), October 5, 2026, §§4–6, Figures 3–6. Question: can an interaction-time grounding signal enable coordination with independently trained partners? MATE appends the partner's previous attention map, transforms it to the receiver's coordinates, and trains object-grounded future-attention alignment alongside other-play. Card task: seven deliberation steps, then private selection among five cards. Forty-eight training seeds there; twelve in each other environment; disjoint policy pairs for cross-play. Card cross-play is 0.823 ± 0.010 SEM versus chance for OP. Removing attention input, grounding, or OP loses the advantage (§6.1). Foraging forces joint collection; Overcooked uses one partially observed layout. Training ablations support component necessity in these settings; they are not acute causal isolation of a frozen live response. Shared internal signals supporting unfamiliar partners is therefore an existing positive precedent. It motivates the competing possibility that direct access makes partners more replaceable rather than intrinsically more dependent.

## Human organization and current LLM systems

[Hollingshead, Cognitive Interdependence and Convergent Expectations in Transactive Memory (2001)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11761309/), original indexed abstract, read during repair 1. A laboratory 2 × 4 factorial controls expected similarity of partner knowledge and four recall incentives defining cognitive interdependence. The abstract reports differentiated memory under different expertise/distinct-recall incentives, and integrated memory under similar expertise/same-recall incentives. This is a causal experimental precedent for the broad organization claim, not merely observational background. Full Methods, sample size, randomization details and the outcome analysis remain unread; the abstract cannot resolve exact overlap. The remaining proposed comparison concerns constant-graph formation topology followed by experienced replacement and targeted handoff repair. Resolving the full original remains necessary before a strong novelty claim.

[Wegner, Erber and Raymond, 1991](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/21380422_Transactive_Memory_in_Close_Relationships), Method and Figure 1, pp. 923–927; full original text accessed through the author-uploaded record. Fifty-nine dating couples were retained or re-paired, with category responsibilities assigned or unassigned; study without discussion, individual recall, then union-of-pair recall. Imposed assignments harm established pairs; the numerical gain for strangers is nonsignificant. Relationship history is not randomized. Neither duplication nor a uniquely relational mechanism is established. The [reviewed case](../../../three_lines_20261025/knowledge/patterns/cases/wegner_1991.md) retains the correction. This supports testing history-by-organization interactions, not assuming their explanation.

[Shang et al., CoThinker, v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.06843v3), §§4–5, Tables 2/4 and Figure 4. Task-specific thinking styles, evolving structured collective memory and moderated reference selection are engineered together. LiveBench/CommonGen-Hard compare system variants and baselines; moderator experiments vary reference count, rewiring and agent number. Question-level bootstrap and paired corrected tests assess benchmark differences. The TMS/style ablation keeps the moderator on; TMS helps some tested backbones and hurts others. This supports bounded utility and coordination-cost findings; attention entropy/perplexity are explicitly diagnostic proxies rather than an identified human-like load mechanism. No role-matched experienced-partner swap or randomized formation-topology comparison was located in the read methods. Detailed benchmark sample-size appendices were not fully audited; no exact total N is claimed.

## Consequential remaining access/search limits

The original [2026 cross-implementation study](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.03644v1) was read through its evaluation definitions, not its full results: implementation variation is another cross-play boundary, not evidence that the proposed formation manipulation is answered. Human interdependence searches identified [Zhang et al. 2007](https://scholars.ln.edu.hk/en/publications/transactive-memory-system-links-work-team-characteristics-and-per/) (104-team observational TMS study, original institutional abstract only), [2025 relative-performance/interdependence experiment](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/09638180.2025.2537650) (methods: additive versus disjunctive reward aggregation), and [a 2026 dissertation on switching interdependence](https://stars.library.ucf.edu/gradstudies_etd_2026/378/) (abstract only). They prevent a broad “interdependence is unstudied” claim. The dissertation and Hollingshead's full methods are unresolved leads before adopting a strong original causal-history claim. The inherited Riedl coordination paper remains limited to its previous documented reading depth.
