# Development decision: responsive evidence acquisition

October 26, 2026. Planner pass with **repair 1 consumed**, independently rechecked; bounded corrections closed. The question comes from the existing responsive-opportunity queue, not a new generation allowance. Source comparisons are in [source-evidence](source-evidence.md).

**Plain question:** When two identical model instances share internal state, does the connection improve their ability to obtain complementary new evidence, their ability to use evidence after obtaining it, or one at the expense of the other?

This matters to the brain-bridge motivation because changing a connection might reorganize how a pair discovers what neither initially knows. More accurate readout from supplied facts would not establish that organization. Conversely, good individual answers could hide duplicated investigations and the loss of facts that could have been discovered.

## First deciding check and its result

The affordable first check was original-source comparison, not outcome mining. The broad proposed contrast fails as a novelty claim: adaptive selection, cooperative active sensing, and interaction-induced loss of exploration already have direct human and computational evidence. Native state exchange does not reset that literature. The closest supplied-source benefit studies also give no reason to assume a useful native return chain exists here.

A narrower comparison survives as an **extension hypothesis**: isolate communication during selection of an external measurement from communication during use of its result, with measurement outcomes unavailable to either process beforehand. This is stronger than counting semantic diversity or treating a replay containing successful future evidence as a nonresponsive generator. It can reveal whether a useful channel moves the bottleneck upstream into evidence acquisition. The extension's flagship value remains unresolved, especially against new-evidence gathering and social-influence literature. We therefore return **conditional as a bounded scientific extension; deferred as a flagship**.

## What would change our assessment

The strongest result would be a stage-specific reversal: the same admitted bridge improves a receiver's use of controlled complementary observations but reduces the pair's production of complementary observations, with both late factual pooling and non-social task competence intact. An acquisition-only intervention must change objectively acquired facts, not merely wording or latent similarity. A delayed-sharing rescue would make the practical consequence clear, but is not itself novel.

An equally important alternative is preserved or improved acquisition and improved use: this would reject the tradeoff in the tested range and establish a working responsive channel for this setting. A broad failure at all stages is a feasibility failure, not evidence that connected cognition intrinsically homogenizes. If an explicit announced sampling plan or a simple copying policy explains the whole effect, that explanation must be reported; it is a valid explanatory answer, not a discovery of self/other machinery.

The strongest concrete extension now uses an instrument-calibration crossover: the same button choice across agents acquires complementary facts in one calibration and duplicates a fact in the other, without changing the hidden code, probe count or joint objective. Early-sharing effects that follow button names differ from effects that follow semantic measurement targets. This adds a falsifiable distinction beyond arbitrary communication topology and final-answer convergence. It remains a conditional proposal after independent logical/source review; the task does not establish its own importance by construction.

## Repair 1: fixed leader, no early values, concealed late assignment

Independent review identified that two-sided choice revision breaks the simple copying predictions, and a post-observation opposite-cue replay could import the otherwise missing bit. The repaired timeline freezes A's commitment after a fresh private availability cue, captures all early live/yoked states before any observation, lets B commit, then reveals values and applies the separately randomized late gate. That late assignment remains hidden throughout acquisition, preventing anticipatory policy changes. The logical check now verifies this stipulated message schema across all hidden codes and both late assignments; it does not certify native-cache isolation. All imported factual values must count in the acquired-information ledger. Effects are defined by B's counterfactual response to A's randomized cue across calibrations, not by action correlation alone. The revised conclusion is still conditional and initially bridge/task specific; ordinary copying-like behavior is an explanatory possibility, not an identified neural code. No additional scientific experiment has been adopted.
